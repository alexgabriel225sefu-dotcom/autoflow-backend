Setup · Lesson 1

Stripe Setup — Your Professional Billing System

Stripe is the world's leading payment infrastructure. Setting it up takes about 30 minutes. After that, every client you onboard enters their card once and Stripe charges them automatically every month forever — without a single action from you.

Why Stripe (Not PayPal, Cash, or Bank Transfer)
1
Automatic monthly charges — clients never forget to pay
With Stripe subscriptions, the charge happens automatically on the same date every month. The client does nothing. You do nothing. The money appears in your bank. Compare this to bank transfer: you must send an invoice, wait for them to log in, hope they remember. 30% of bank transfer invoices are paid late. With Stripe, 0% are late because Stripe charges the card automatically.
2
Professional invoices and receipts — tax compliant
Stripe automatically emails a professional receipt to clients after every charge. The receipt includes your business name, the service description, the amount, and a receipt number. This is tax-compliant documentation that both you and the client can use for their accounting. You never have to create or send invoices manually.
3
Client card stored securely — you never see the number
Stripe is PCI-compliant. Client card numbers are tokenized and stored on Stripe's servers — not yours, not your database, nowhere you control. You never have access to the actual card number. This protects you from liability and protects the client's financial data. It is a major trust signal: "I don't store your card — Stripe does, and they protect it."
4
Chargeback protection — evidence-based dispute handling
When a client disputes a charge (rare but it happens), Stripe's dispute management tool lets you submit evidence: email records, signed proposals, delivery confirmations. Stripe presents your evidence to the card network. Agencies with good records win 70%+ of disputes. PayPal and bank transfers give you almost no protection in disputes.
Step 1 — Create Your Stripe Account
5
Go to stripe.com and click Start now
Open stripe.com in your browser. You see the Stripe homepage with a dark background and the headline "Financial infrastructure for the internet." In the top right corner, you see a button labeled Start now — it is a purple/dark button. Click it.
You land on a sign-up page with two fields: Email address and Password. Enter your business email (not a personal email — use the one you use for client communication). Create a strong password. Click Create account. Stripe sends a verification email immediately.
6
Verify your email address
Open your email inbox. Find the email from Stripe with subject "Verify your email address." Click the Verify email address button inside it. Your browser opens and you land directly inside the Stripe Dashboard — a dark interface with a left sidebar and your revenue summary in the center (showing $0.00 since you're new).
7
Activate your account — required to receive real money
At the top of the Stripe Dashboard, you see a yellow banner that reads "Activate your account to start accepting payments." Click the Activate your account button on the right side of that banner. You enter a multi-step onboarding flow.
Step 1 — Business type: Select Individual if you are operating as a sole trader/freelancer. Select Company if you have a registered business entity. Most beginners choose Individual. Click Continue.

Step 2 — Personal details: Enter your legal first name, last name, date of birth, and home address (Stripe uses this for identity verification, not public display). Click Continue.

Step 3 — Business details: Enter your country, and for "Business website" you can enter your agency website URL or your LinkedIn profile URL if you don't have a website yet. Business description: type "AI automation services for small businesses." Click Continue.

Step 4 — Bank account: Enter your bank account number and routing/sort code for payouts. Stripe deposits your revenue here every 2–7 business days (depending on your country). Click Continue.

Step 5 — Identity verification: Stripe asks you to upload a government-issued ID — passport or national ID card. Take a photo and upload both sides. Verification takes 1–3 business days. You can still set up products and test everything while waiting.
Step 2 — Create Your Service Products in Stripe
8
Navigate to the Products section
In the Stripe Dashboard left sidebar, find and click Product catalog — it is listed under the "Billing" section. The page shows an empty product catalog with a header and an + Add product button in the top right. Click + Add product.
9
Create Product 1: Basic Automation Package
A product creation form appears. Fill in:
Name: Basic Automation Package
Description: Monthly maintenance of one AI automation (WhatsApp bot or Instagram bot)

Under "Pricing," click + Add a price. A pricing form appears:
— Billing period: select Monthly (click the dropdown)
— Price: type 150
— Currency: USD (or your local currency — click the currency field to change it)

Click Save product in the top right. The product is saved and you return to the catalog. You now see it listed.
10
Create Product 2: Professional Package — $300/month
Click + Add product again. Name: Professional Package. Description: Monthly management of WhatsApp bot + Instagram DM bot + monthly performance report. Price: recurring monthly, $300. Save. Repeat for:
Product 3: Premium Package — $500/month — all automations + lead follow-up system + CRM integration + weekly reports
Product 4: Setup Fee — set the price type to One time (not recurring) — leave the price at $1 for now; you will enter a custom amount for each client when needed
11
Copy and save every Price ID
After creating each product, click on its name in the Product Catalog to open its detail page. Under the "Pricing" section, each price is listed with a Price ID — a string that starts with price_ followed by random characters (e.g. price_1OaB3kLkdIwHu7ix3mfGq8Lc).
Click the copy icon next to each Price ID. Paste all four Price IDs into a Google Doc or note — label them clearly (Basic $150, Professional $300, Premium $500, Setup Fee). You will need these exact IDs when creating subscriptions and when building Make.com automations. Without the Price ID you cannot create a subscription programmatically.
Step 3 — Explore the Stripe Dashboard
12
Understand each section of the Dashboard
In the left sidebar, here is what each section does:
Home: your revenue summary — total volume today, this week, MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)
Customers: every client who has paid you — click any customer to see all their payments and subscriptions
Product catalog: your service packages and prices
Subscriptions: all active recurring billing relationships — shows who is paying, how much, and next billing date
Payments: full history of every charge, successful or failed
Billing → Overview: MRR graph, churn rate, active subscription count — your monthly health dashboard
⚠ Test Mode vs Live Mode
Stripe has two modes: Test Mode and Live Mode. In Test Mode, no real money moves — it is a sandbox. While waiting for account approval, build and test everything in Test Mode. When your account is activated, toggle to Live Mode using the switch at the top left of the Dashboard. Important: products and prices created in Test Mode do NOT transfer to Live Mode. You must recreate them. To test in Test Mode, use Stripe's test card: 4242 4242 4242 4242, any future expiry date, and any 3-digit CVV.

Billing · Lesson 2

Create Monthly Subscriptions for Clients

When a client agrees to your proposal, you need to collect the setup fee and start the monthly subscription. This lesson shows two methods — the quick 2-minute Payment Link method and the professional Invoice + Subscription method — and explains when to use each.

Method A — Payment Link (Easiest, Fastest)
A Payment Link is a URL that opens a Stripe-hosted checkout page. The client clicks it, enters their card details, and submits. Stripe starts the subscription. No code required. Takes 2 minutes to set up. Best for new clients and for getting started fast.
1
Navigate to Payment Links in Stripe
In the Stripe Dashboard left sidebar, scroll down and click Payment links. It is listed under the "Products" section. You see a list of any existing links (empty if this is your first). Click the + New payment link button in the top right — it is a purple button.
2
Select the product and configure the link
A configuration screen appears. Under "Add line item," click Add a product. A search box appears — type the name of the product (e.g., "Professional Package"). It appears in the dropdown. Click it. The price ($300/month recurring) fills in automatically. Quantity stays at 1.
On the right side of the screen, you see a live preview of the checkout page as it will appear to your client. Review it: your business name appears at the top, the product name and price are shown, and there is a card entry form below.
3
Configure customer data collection
Scroll down on the configuration page. Find the Customer information section. Make sure Email address is toggled ON — Stripe will collect the client's email, which you need to know who paid. You can also enable "Phone number" if useful.
Under "After payment," set the confirmation page text: "Thank you — your automation service is now active. You will receive a welcome email from us within 24 hours with next steps." This reassures the client immediately after payment.
4
Create and copy the link
Click Create link in the top right. Stripe generates a URL that looks like https://buy.stripe.com/xxxxxxxxxxxx. Click Copy next to the link. This link is permanent — you can use it for any client on the Professional Package. Save it in your Google Doc next to the Professional Package Price ID.
5
Send the link to the client
Send this exact message via WhatsApp or email immediately after the client agrees on the call:
Hi [Name], great chatting earlier! To get started, here's the link to set up your monthly service: [Stripe Payment Link] Just enter your card details — takes about 30 seconds. Your card will be charged $300 on the [date] of every month and you can cancel anytime with no penalty. Once payment goes through, I'll email you with next steps and we'll have your automation live within 7 days. Any questions, just message me here.
The client clicks the link, sees a professional Stripe-branded checkout (not a sketchy form), enters their Visa or Mastercard details, and clicks Pay. Stripe charges them immediately for the first month. You receive an email notification. The subscription starts. Stripe automatically charges the card on the same date every month from that point forward.
Method B — Stripe Invoice + Subscription (Most Professional)
Use this method for larger clients, enterprise clients, or clients who prefer to receive a formal invoice before entering card details. This method goes through Stripe's full subscription creation flow.
6
Create the client as a Customer in Stripe
In the Stripe Dashboard, click Customers in the left sidebar. Click + Add customer in the top right. A form appears. Fill in:
Email: the client's email address
Name: the business owner's full name
Description: their business name (e.g., "Bella Vista Restaurant")
Phone: optional but useful

Click Add customer. The customer record is created. You now see their profile page with their name at the top and empty payment history below.
7
Create a subscription for this customer
On the customer's profile page, in the top right area, click the Actions dropdown button. Select Create subscription from the dropdown menu. A subscription creation form appears.
Under "Add product," click the search field and type the product name (e.g., Professional Package). Select it. The $300/month price appears. Set:
Billing cycle start: today (first charge today, recurring on this same date monthly)
Send invoice to customer: toggle this ON — Stripe emails them a professional invoice with a secure card entry link

Click Start subscription at the top right.
8
What happens after you click Start subscription
Stripe immediately sends the client an email with subject "Invoice from [Your Agency Name]." The email contains a professional invoice showing the service name, amount ($300), and a prominent button: Pay Invoice. The client clicks it, lands on a secure Stripe-hosted payment page, enters their card, and clicks Pay.
After they pay: Stripe charges their card immediately, stores the card securely, sends them a receipt, and automatically charges the same card on the same date every subsequent month. You receive an email notification confirming the payment. The client's record in your Stripe Dashboard updates to show "Active" subscription.
9
Collect the Setup Fee separately
The setup fee is a one-time charge — separate from the monthly retainer. To collect it: go to Customers → click the client → click Actions → Create invoice. Add the "Setup Fee" product. Set the price to the exact amount you quoted (e.g., $600). Toggle "Send invoice automatically" ON. Click Send invoice.
Critical rule: collect the setup fee before you begin building. The setup fee compensates your time for building the system. The monthly retainer compensates your time for maintaining it. Never start building until the setup fee is paid. Include this in every proposal: "I begin building immediately upon receipt of the setup fee."
✓ Which Method to Use When
Use Payment Links for all clients up to your first 10. They are fast, professional, and require zero setup beyond creating the link once. Switch to Method B (Customer + Subscription) when you want a proper customer record in Stripe with full history, or when an enterprise client requires a formal invoice before entering card details.
✦ Exercise — Create All 3 Payment Links
Create a Payment Link for each of your three recurring products: Basic ($150), Professional ($300), and Premium ($500). Save all three links in your Google Doc. Then in Test Mode, send yourself all three links and go through the checkout on each one using the test card 4242 4242 4242 4242. Verify that each subscription appears correctly in your Stripe Test Mode dashboard. Now you are ready to send these links to real clients the moment they say yes.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 2
Send the Payment Link immediately after the client agrees — while they are still in the buying mindset. Every hour you wait, the chance of them paying decreases.
Collect the setup fee before starting any work. No exceptions.
Stripe automatically handles card storage, monthly charges, receipts, and failed payment retries — you manage none of this manually.
Use Method B (Customer + Invoice) for enterprise clients who need formal documentation for their accounts department.
Automation · Lesson 3

Automate Your Billing Workflows with Stripe Webhooks

Stripe webhooks send a notification to Make.com every time a billing event happens — new subscription, successful payment, failed payment, cancellation. Make.com then triggers the right automated workflow: onboarding email, payment recovery, offboarding. Every billing event handled automatically.

Step 1 — Get Your Make.com Webhook URL
1
Create a new Make.com Scenario for Stripe webhooks
In Make.com, click Scenarios → + Create a new scenario. Click the starting circle. Search for Webhooks. Click the purple Webhooks logo. Select Custom webhook. A panel appears — click Add to create a new webhook. Name it Stripe — New Subscription. Click Save.
Make.com generates a webhook URL — it looks like https://hook.eu1.make.com/abcdef123456. Click Copy address to clipboard. You will paste this into Stripe in the next steps. Keep this Make.com browser tab open.
Step 2 — Register the Webhook in Stripe
2
Open Stripe Dashboard → Developers → Webhooks
In the Stripe Dashboard, click Developers in the top right corner — it is a small text link, not a sidebar item. This opens the Developer panel. In the left submenu that appears, click Webhooks. You see a Webhooks page with an + Add endpoint button. Click it.
3
Configure the webhook endpoint
An "Add endpoint" form appears. In the Endpoint URL field, paste the Make.com webhook URL you copied. In the Description field, type: "Make.com — Stripe automation workflows."
Click + Select events to choose which Stripe events trigger this webhook. A searchable list of all Stripe events appears. Search for and check each of these:
✓ customer.subscription.created
✓ customer.subscription.deleted
✓ payment_intent.payment_failed

Click Add events. Then click Add endpoint to save.
4
Copy the Webhook Signing Secret
After saving, you land on the webhook endpoint detail page. You see a section called Signing secret with a hidden value and a Reveal button. Click Reveal. The secret appears — it starts with whsec_. Click Copy.
Save this in your Google Doc. This secret is used to verify that webhook calls genuinely come from Stripe and not from someone spoofing the endpoint. In Make.com you don't need to configure this separately — Make.com handles it automatically — but if you ever connect Stripe webhooks to a custom server, you would verify the signature using this secret.
Step 3 — Build Make.com Scenarios for Each Event
5
Scenario 1: New Subscription → Welcome Email + CRM Update
Back in Make.com, your Webhook trigger scenario is waiting. After the Webhooks module, add a Router module (search "Router" — it splits one flow into multiple paths). Create Route 1 with a filter: the event type from the Stripe webhook equals customer.subscription.created.
On Route 1, add Gmail → Send an Email. To: the client email from the webhook payload. Subject: "Welcome — your automation service is now live!" Use this template:
Subject: Welcome — your automation service is now live! Hi [Client First Name], Payment confirmed — thank you! I'm excited to get started on your automation system. Here's what happens next: → I'll begin building your [Automation Type] today → You'll receive a progress update within 48 hours → Your system will be fully live within 7 days If you have any questions before then, just reply to this email or message me on WhatsApp. Looking forward to delivering great results for [Business Name]! [Your Name] [Your Agency]
After Gmail, add Google Sheets → Add a Row to log the new client in your CRM Client Tracker sheet. Map: Date = today, Client Name, Business, Package, Monthly Amount, Status = Active.
6
Scenario 2: Failed Payment → Recovery Email
In the Router, add Route 2 with filter: event type equals payment_intent.payment_failed. On this route, first add a Sleep module (search "Sleep") — set it to wait 2 hours. This gives the client time to notice the issue themselves before you email them. After Sleep, add Gmail → Send an Email:
Subject: Quick heads up about your payment — [Business Name] Hi [Name], Just a friendly note — your payment for this month didn't go through (this usually happens when a card expires or the bank blocks an automated charge). No worries at all — it happens. To update your payment details, click here: [Stripe Customer Portal Link] It takes less than 60 seconds and your service continues without interruption. Let me know if you need any help! [Your Name]
After Gmail, add Google Sheets → Update a Row: find this client's row in the CRM and set a "Payment Issue" flag so you can follow up personally if they don't resolve it within 48 hours.
7
Scenario 3: Subscription Cancelled → Offboarding + Alert
Router Route 3: filter on event type equals customer.subscription.deleted. Add Gmail → Send an Email to the client:
Subject: Thank you for working with us — [Business Name] Hi [Name], I've received confirmation that your subscription has been cancelled. I hope we were able to make a positive impact on your business during our time working together. Could I ask — was there anything we could have done better? Your feedback helps me improve the service for other clients. Just hit reply with a sentence or two if you have a moment. If you'd like to restart your automation service in the future, we're always here. Just reach out. Wishing you and [Business Name] all the best, [Your Name]
After the client email, add Gmail → Send an Email to yourself. Subject: "ALERT — [Business Name] cancelled." Body: include the client name, their package, and the monthly amount lost. Also add Google Sheets → Update a Row: set this client's CRM status to "Churned" and note the date.
Step 4 — Your Monthly Revenue Dashboard
8
Access Stripe's built-in MRR dashboard
In the Stripe Dashboard left sidebar, click Billing → Overview. This page shows your Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) as a number at the top, with a graph of its growth over time. Below it: number of active subscriptions, new subscriptions this month, cancelled subscriptions, and net revenue change.
Check this page once a week — ideally every Monday morning. Your goal milestones:
$1,500 MRR = 5 clients on Professional ($300)
$3,000 MRR = 10 clients on Professional — this is your first major milestone
$5,000 MRR = mix of packages adding up to $5,000
$10,000 MRR = the $10K/month target covered in Module 14
✓ The Complete Billing System — What You've Built
You now have a fully automated billing operation: client pays → subscription created → welcome email sent → monthly charges collected automatically → failed payments trigger a friendly recovery email → cancellations trigger a professional offboarding sequence with an alert to you → CRM stays updated throughout. You will never manually send an invoice, chase a payment, or forget to follow up on a failed charge again.

Key Takeaways — Lesson 3
Set up webhooks for all three key events: subscription created, payment failed, subscription cancelled. Each one triggers a different automated response.
The 2-hour delay before the failed payment email is intentional — it gives clients time to notice and fix it themselves, making your email less alarming when it arrives.
Professional cancellation handling with a genuine feedback request converts 20–30% of cancellations into future revenue.
Check the Stripe Billing Overview once a week. $3,000 MRR (10 clients at $300) is your first major milestone. Work backwards from it: how many sales calls per week do you need to hit it?
