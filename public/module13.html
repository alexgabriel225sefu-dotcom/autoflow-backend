Setup · Lesson 1

Stripe Setup — Your Professional Billing System

Stripe is the world's leading payment platform. Setting it up correctly from day one means you can accept credit cards from any country, charge monthly retainers automatically, and look like a professional agency from your very first client.

Step 1 — Create Your Stripe Account
1
Go to stripe.com and click "Start now"
Open stripe.com. Click the Start now button. Enter your email and create a password. Click Create account.
2
Verify your email
Stripe sends a verification email. Click the link in it. You are taken to the Stripe Dashboard.
3
Complete your business profile
In the Stripe Dashboard, click Activate your account or go to Settings → Business settings. Fill in your business details:
Business type: Individual or Company — if you are just starting, Individual works fine
Business name: Your agency name
Business website: Your server URL if you don't have a website yet
Business category: Select "Computer Programming, Data Processing" or "Consulting"
Description: "AI automation services for small businesses"

Fill in your personal details and bank account information to receive payouts. Stripe needs this to verify your identity and deposit money to you.
4
Get your API keys
In the Stripe Dashboard, click Developers in the top right → click API keys. You see two keys:
Publishable key — starts with pk_live_... — used in your frontend (website). Safe to expose publicly.
Secret key — starts with sk_live_... — used in your server. NEVER expose this publicly.

Copy both. Add the Secret key to your Render environment variables as STRIPE_SECRET_KEY.
Step 2 — Create Your Retainer Products in Stripe
1
Go to Products in Stripe Dashboard
In the Stripe Dashboard left sidebar, click Product catalog. Click + Add product.
2
Create a product for each retainer tier
Create one product per service tier. Here is the setup for a Basic WhatsApp Bot retainer:
Name: WhatsApp Bot — Monthly Maintenance
Description: Monthly maintenance and optimization of your AI WhatsApp bot
Pricing: Click "Add a price" → Recurring → Monthly → $200 (or your actual price)
Currency: USD (or your local currency)

Click Save product. Repeat for each tier: $150, $200, $300, $400 monthly retainers.
3
Copy the Price ID for each product
After saving each product, click on it to open it. Under "Pricing," you see the price listed with a Price ID that starts with price_. Copy this ID. You will use it when creating subscriptions for clients.
⚠️ Test Mode vs Live Mode
Stripe has a Test Mode and a Live Mode. In Test Mode, no real money moves. Use Test Mode to set everything up and verify it works. When ready for real clients, toggle to Live Mode (top left of the dashboard). All your products and prices need to be recreated in Live Mode — they do not transfer automatically from Test Mode.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 1
Complete your Stripe business profile and bank account before charging any real clients.
Secret key goes in Render environment variables only. Never put it in frontend code.
Create one product per retainer tier — $150, $200, $300, $400/month. Copy the Price IDs.
Always test in Test Mode first. Stripe provides test card numbers: 4242 4242 4242 4242 with any future date and any CVV.
Billing · Lesson 2

Create Monthly Subscriptions for Clients

Once a client agrees to the monthly retainer, you create a Stripe subscription. They enter their card once and Stripe charges them automatically every month. You never have to ask for payment again.

Method 1 — Stripe Payment Link (Simplest)
The fastest way to collect the first payment and set up recurring billing. Send the client a link — they click, enter their card, done.
1
Create a Payment Link in Stripe
In Stripe Dashboard, click Payment links in the left sidebar. Click + New payment link.
2
Configure the payment link
Product: Click "Add a product" → select the retainer product you created (e.g., WhatsApp Bot Monthly Maintenance $200)
After payment: Set the confirmation page to show "Your automation service is now active. You will receive an email within 24 hours."
Collect customer details: Enable "Email" — so you know who paid

Click Create link. Copy the URL.
3
Send to the client
Send the payment link to the client via WhatsApp or email: "To activate your monthly service, please complete the payment here: [link]. Your card will be charged $200 on the same date every month and you can cancel anytime."
The client clicks the link, enters their Visa or Mastercard details, and submits. Stripe processes the first payment immediately and then automatically charges the card on the same date every month. No action needed from you.
Method 2 — Stripe Customer Portal (Most Professional)
1
Enable the Customer Portal in Stripe
In Stripe Dashboard → Settings → Billing → Customer portal. Toggle it ON. Configure what clients can do: view invoices, update payment method, cancel subscription (you can disable cancellation if you want).
2
Create a customer and subscription via Stripe API
When a client agrees to the retainer, create them in Stripe using the API. Add this endpoint to your server.js:
app.post('/api/billing/create-subscription', auth, async (req, res) => { const { clientEmail, clientName, priceId } = req.body; const stripe = require('stripe')(process.env.STRIPE_SECRET_KEY); try { // Create customer const customer = await stripe.customers.create({ email: clientEmail, name: clientName }); // Create a checkout session for them to enter card const session = await stripe.checkout.sessions.create({ customer: customer.id, payment_method_types: ['card'], line_items: [{ price: priceId, quantity: 1 }], mode: 'subscription', success_url: 'https://aicashsystem.onrender.com/billing-success.html', cancel_url: 'https://aicashsystem.onrender.com/billing-cancel.html' }); res.json({ checkoutUrl: session.url }); } catch(e) { res.status(500).json({ error: e.message }); } });
3
Send the checkout URL to the client
Call this endpoint from your app to get the checkout URL. Send it to the client. They go through Stripe's professional checkout flow, enter their card, and the subscription starts immediately.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 2
Payment Links are the simplest option — create once, send to client, done in 2 minutes.
Always send the payment link immediately after the client agrees on the call — while they are still in the buying mindset.
Stripe automatically sends receipts to clients and retries failed payments — you never have to chase.
For professional onboarding use Stripe Checkout Sessions — they provide a branded, secure payment experience.
Automation · Lesson 3

Automate Your Billing Workflows with Stripe Webhooks

Connect Stripe webhooks to Make.com to automate every billing event — new subscriptions trigger onboarding, failed payments trigger follow-up emails, cancellations trigger offboarding. Everything automatic.

Step 1 — Set Up Stripe Webhooks
1
Go to Stripe Dashboard → Developers → Webhooks
In the Stripe Dashboard, click Developers in the top right. Click Webhooks in the left menu. Click + Add endpoint.
2
Add your server webhook URL
In the "Endpoint URL" field, enter your Render server URL with /stripe-webhook:
https://aicashsystem.onrender.com/stripe-webhook
3
Select events to listen for
Click Select events. Add these events:
✓ customer.subscription.created — new client subscribed
✓ invoice.payment_succeeded — monthly payment collected
✓ invoice.payment_failed — payment failed (card expired etc.)
✓ customer.subscription.deleted — client cancelled

Click Add endpoint. Copy the Signing secret that appears — add it to Render as STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET.
Step 2 — Build Make.com Automations for Each Billing Event
1
Automation 1 — New Subscription → Welcome Email
When customer.subscription.created fires, your server receives it and can forward to Make.com. Build a Make.com scenario that: sends the client a welcome email with their access code and next steps, adds them to your Client Tracker sheet, creates a reminder for you to start building their automation.
2
Automation 2 — Failed Payment → Recovery Email
When invoice.payment_failed fires, automatically send the client an email asking them to update their payment method. Include a direct link to the Stripe Customer Portal where they can update their card in 30 seconds.
The email should be friendly, not alarming: "Hi [Name], just a quick heads up — your payment for this month did not go through (possibly an expired card). Click here to update your payment method: [Stripe Portal Link]. Your service continues normally while you do this."
3
Automation 3 — Monthly Payment → Update Revenue Tracker
When invoice.payment_succeeded fires, automatically update your revenue tracking sheet. This gives you a real-time view of monthly recurring revenue without manually counting payments.
4
Automation 4 — Cancellation → Offboarding Email
When customer.subscription.deleted fires, send a professional offboarding email: thank them for working with you, ask for feedback (what made them cancel?), offer to restart at any time, and remind them you are available for one-off projects.
Why this matters: 30% of cancellations come back within 6 months if handled professionally. A good offboarding email converts cancellations into future revenue.
✓ The Complete Billing System
You now have a fully automated billing operation: client pays → subscription created → welcome email sent → monthly payments collected automatically → failed payments recovered → cancellations handled professionally. You never manually send an invoice or chase a payment again. This is what a real agency operation looks like.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 3
Set up Stripe webhooks for all 4 key events: subscription created, payment succeeded, payment failed, subscription cancelled.
Failed payment recovery emails recover 40–60% of failed payments — always automate this.
Professional cancellation handling converts 30% of cancellations into future revenue.
Add STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET to Render environment variables — without it, Stripe webhooks will be rejected by your server.
