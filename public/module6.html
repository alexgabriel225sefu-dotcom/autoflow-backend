Setup · Lesson 1

Instagram Graph API — Complete Setup

Instagram DM automation runs through the Meta Graph API — the same system that powers Facebook Messenger bots. You need a Facebook Developer App, a Business Instagram account, and a linked Facebook Page. Follow every step exactly and you will have API access in about 30 minutes. Do not skip any step — each one is required.

Before You Start — 4 Requirements
Check these four things right now. If any one is missing, the setup will not work at all.
1
Instagram Business account (not Personal, not Creator)
Open Instagram on your phone. Go to the profile. Tap the three-line menu ☰ in the top right. Tap Settings and privacy. Scroll down and tap Account type and tools. If it says "Switch to Professional Account" — the account is still Personal. Tap it, choose Business (not Creator), select a business category, and tap Done. The account is now a Business account.
Why does this matter? Only Instagram Business accounts can connect to the Messenger API. Personal and Creator accounts cannot be automated — the API simply rejects them. If a client has a Creator account, they must switch it to Business before you can build the bot.
2
Connect Instagram to a Facebook Page
Open Facebook on a desktop browser and log in. Click your profile picture at the top right. Click Pages. Select the Facebook Page for the business. On the left sidebar click Settings. Scroll down and click Linked Accounts. Click Connect account next to Instagram. A popup opens — log in to the Instagram Business account. Click Confirm. The Page and Instagram account are now linked.
If no Facebook Page exists yet: on facebook.com click Create at the top left → click Page → type the business name → choose a category → click Create Page. This takes 2 minutes. Then go back and link Instagram.
3
A Facebook account that manages the Page
You need login access to the Facebook account that is the Admin of that Page. You will use this account to generate the API access token. If you are setting this up for a client, they need to be with you (in person or on a screen share) for the token generation step — they must log in to their own Facebook account during setup.
4
Register as a Facebook Developer (free, takes 2 minutes)
Open a new browser tab and go to developers.facebook.com. Click Log In at the top right and log in with your own personal Facebook account. If a banner appears saying "Register as a Developer" — click it and click Register. Accept the terms. You are now a registered Facebook Developer. This is free and permanent.
Use your own Facebook account for the Developer account — not the client's. You will be the one building and managing the app, so it lives under your developer account.
Create the Facebook App — Step by Step
1
Go to developers.facebook.com and click "My Apps"
At developers.facebook.com, look at the top right corner. You see a button labeled My Apps. Click it. A page opens showing all your developer apps — it may be empty if this is your first time. You are now in the App Dashboard area.
2
Click the green "Create App" button
On the My Apps page, find and click the green Create App button in the top right corner. A setup wizard opens. The first screen asks what type of app you want to build — you see several options listed as cards.
3
Select "Business" as the app type and click Next
Click the card labeled Business. A blue border appears around it to confirm your selection. Click Next at the bottom right of the wizard.
The Business app type unlocks the Instagram Messaging API and Pages API — which are exactly what you need. Other types (Consumer, Gaming) do not include these APIs.
4
Fill in the app name, email, and create
The next screen has three fields. Fill them in:
App display name: Type something like DM Bot — [Client Business Name]

App contact email: Your email address

Business Account: If you have a Meta Business portfolio select it. If not, look for a small link that says "I don't want to connect a business portfolio yet" and click it.

Click Create App. Facebook shows a security check — re-enter your Facebook password and click Submit. The App Dashboard opens.
5
Add Instagram as a product to your app
You are now on the App Dashboard. In the main area you see a list of "Add Products to Your App." Scroll until you find Instagram (it shows the rainbow gradient Instagram icon). Click the Set up button below it. The page reloads and "Instagram" appears in the left sidebar under Products.
If you don't see Instagram in the list, scroll to the very bottom and look for a "Show More" button or use the search field. You can also click the + icon next to "Products" in the left sidebar to browse all available products.
6
Navigate to Instagram API Setup and add the Instagram account
In the left sidebar, click Instagram to expand it. Click API Setup with Instagram Login or Basic Display — whichever is shown. On that page, find the section for adding Instagram testers. Click Add Instagram Testers. In the field that appears, type the Instagram Business account username. Click Submit.
After submitting, the account owner must accept the tester invitation from their Instagram app. On the phone: open Instagram → Settings → Apps and Websites → Tester Invites → tap Accept next to your app name. Once accepted the account is connected and ready.
Generate the Long-Lived Access Token
1
Open the Graph API Explorer in a new tab
Open a new browser tab and go to developers.facebook.com/tools/explorer. This is Meta's official API testing tool. Make sure you are still logged in with the Facebook account that manages the Page. You see a large text field on the left and a response panel on the right.
2
Select your app from the "Meta App" dropdown
In the top right of the Graph API Explorer, find the dropdown labeled Meta App. Click it. A list of your apps appears. Click the app you just created (the DM Bot app). The explorer is now connected to your app.
3
Add required permissions and generate access token
Click Add a Permission. A dropdown appears with hundreds of permissions. Search for and add each of these four:
instagram_manage_messages — read and send DMs
pages_messaging — messaging via Pages
instagram_basic — basic account access
pages_show_list — list connected Pages

After adding all four permissions, click the blue Generate Access Token button. A Facebook login popup appears. Click Continue as [Your Name] then click Done. The Access Token field now shows a long string starting with "EAA..." — this is your short-lived token.
4
Convert the short-lived token to a 60-day long-lived token
Copy the entire token from the Access Token field. Now open a new browser tab. In the address bar paste this URL — replacing the three placeholders with your real values:
https://graph.facebook.com/v18.0/oauth/access_token?grant_type=fb_exchange_token&client_id=YOUR_APP_ID&client_secret=YOUR_APP_SECRET&fb_exchange_token=SHORT_LIVED_TOKEN
YOUR_APP_ID: In your Facebook App → Settings → Basic → copy the "App ID" at the top
YOUR_APP_SECRET: Same page → "App Secret" → click Show → copy the revealed value
SHORT_LIVED_TOKEN: The token you just copied from Graph API Explorer

Press Enter. The browser shows JSON like: {"access_token":"EAAB...","token_type":"bearer"}. Copy the long string after "access_token":". This is your Long-Lived Token — it lasts 60 days. Paste it into a secure Notes doc immediately — you need it in Lesson 2.
5
Get your Page ID and Instagram Account ID
Go back to the Graph API Explorer. In the query field replace the text with me/accounts and click Submit. The response shows your Facebook Pages. Find the Page linked to the Instagram account. Copy its id value — this is the Page ID. Then type {PAGE_ID}?fields=instagram_business_account (replace with your real Page ID) and click Submit. Copy the instagram_business_account id from the response. Save both IDs to your notes.
⚠ Token Expiry — Set a Reminder Right Now
Long-lived tokens expire after 60 days. Open your calendar right now and set a recurring reminder for every 55 days labeled "Renew Instagram API token for [Client]." To renew: call the same long-lived URL from Step 4 again with the existing token and you get a fresh 60-day token. Add this as a $50/month "maintenance" item to every client retainer.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 1
Only Instagram Business accounts (not Personal, not Creator) can be connected to the API — switch the account type first.
Always convert the short-lived token to a long-lived token immediately — the short one expires in 1–2 hours.
Save 5 values in a secure doc: App ID, App Secret, Long-Lived Token, Page ID, Instagram Account ID.
Set a calendar reminder every 55 days to renew the token before it expires and breaks the bot.

Step 1 — Create the Make.com Webhook
1
Go to Make.com and create a new Scenario
Open make.com and log in. Click + Create a new scenario in the top right. A blank scenario editor opens. You see a large empty circle in the center — this is where the trigger goes. Click the circle.
2
Search "Webhooks" and select Custom Webhook
The app search panel opens on the right. Type Webhooks in the search field. The "Webhooks" app appears — click it. A list of actions appears. Click Custom Webhook. A settings panel opens on the right side.
3
Click "Add" to create a new webhook, name it, and save
In the webhook settings panel, click Add. A small form appears. In the "Webhook name" field type Instagram DM Bot — [Client Name]. Click Save. Make.com generates a unique webhook URL — it looks like https://hook.eu1.make.com/abc123xyz.... Click Copy address to clipboard. Paste this URL into your notes — you need it in the next step.
After creating the webhook, click OK to close the settings. The webhook trigger now appears in your scenario as a circle. Click Run once at the bottom left — Make.com is now actively listening for the first incoming request.
4
Register the webhook in your Facebook App (Callback URL)
Switch back to your Facebook App dashboard at developers.facebook.com. In the left sidebar, find and click Webhooks. Click Add Callback URL. Two fields appear. In the first field paste your Make.com webhook URL. In the second field type any word — for example verify123. Remember this word. Click Verify and Save.
Meta sends a verification GET request to your Make.com URL. Make.com receives it and confirms the webhook is real. If verification fails: make sure Make.com was set to "Run once" before clicking Verify and Save, and that you copied the full webhook URL correctly (no missing characters).
5
Subscribe to Instagram "messages" webhook event
After the callback URL is verified (you see a green checkmark), find the Instagram section in the Webhooks page. Look for a list of subscribable fields. Find messages in the list and check the checkbox next to it. Click Subscribe. Meta will now send a notification to your Make.com webhook every time a DM arrives.
6
Send a test DM to capture the data structure
From a different Instagram account (your personal account works), send a DM to the Instagram Business account. Go back to Make.com — the scenario should have captured the incoming data. Click on the webhook circle to see the data. You will see the full JSON structure that Meta sends. Find and note the exact paths to the message text and sender ID.
The message text is at: entry[0].messaging[0].message.text
The sender's Instagram ID is at: entry[0].messaging[0].sender.id

These are the two variables you use in the next steps. Every Make.com variable picker will show the exact path after you have captured this real test message.
Step 2 — Add GPT-4o to Write the Reply
1
Click "+" after the Webhook and add OpenAI
Hover over the webhook circle and click the small + that appears to the right of it. The app search opens. Type OpenAI and click the OpenAI app. Click Create a Chat Completion. In the settings, click Add next to Connection and paste your OpenAI API key. Click Save. Set the Model to gpt-4o.
2
Set the System message — the bot's personality and instructions
In the OpenAI settings, find the Messages section. Click Add item. Set Role to system. In the Content field paste this template, then customize the bracketed parts for the client:
You are the friendly AI assistant of [BUSINESS NAME] on Instagram. Your job is to reply to Instagram DMs in a warm, natural tone that matches the brand's voice. About the business: - Name: [BUSINESS NAME] - What we offer: [PRODUCTS OR SERVICES] - Price range: [PRICE RANGE] - Location/delivery: [LOCATION OR DELIVERY INFO] - How to order or book: [ORDERING PROCESS] - Instagram handle: @[HANDLE] Rules: - Keep replies to 1–3 sentences — Instagram is casual - Sound like a real person, not a robot - If asked about prices, give the range and invite them to DM for a custom quote - Always end with a question or suggestion to keep the conversation going - Never invent specific prices, dates, or stock levels - Reply in the same language the customer used
3
Add the User message using the DM text variable
Click Add item again. Set Role to user. In the Content field click the variable picker icon (it looks like a small map pin). Navigate to the Webhook module → entry[] → messaging[] → message → text. Click it to insert it. This passes the actual DM text to GPT-4o as the "user's message."
If the variable path shows as a long array reference like {{1.entry[].messaging[].message.text}} — that is correct. Click OK to close the OpenAI settings.
Step 3 — Send the AI Reply Back via Instagram
1
Add an HTTP module after OpenAI
Hover over the OpenAI circle and click +. Search HTTP. Click the HTTP app. Click Make a Request. The HTTP settings panel opens on the right.
2
Configure the HTTP request — URL and Method
Fill in the HTTP module settings exactly as shown:
URL:
https://graph.facebook.com/v18.0/me/messages
Method: Select POST from the dropdown

Query String — click "Add item" and add this parameter:
Name: access_token
Value: paste your Long-Lived Access Token from Lesson 1

Body type: Select Raw
Content type: Select JSON (application/json)
3
Set the JSON request body with sender ID and AI reply
In the Body field paste this JSON. Use the variable picker to replace the variable references:
{ "recipient": { "id": "{{1.entry[].messaging[].sender.id}}" }, "message": { "text": "{{2.choices[].message.content}}" }, "messaging_type": "RESPONSE" }
{{1.entry[].messaging[].sender.id}} — click variable picker → Webhooks module → entry[] → messaging[] → sender → id
{{2.choices[].message.content}} — click variable picker → OpenAI module → choices[] → message → content

Click OK to save the HTTP settings.
4
Turn the scenario ON and send a real test DM
At the bottom left of the Make.com scenario editor, find the toggle labeled Scheduling. Click it to turn it ON. The scenario is now live. From your personal Instagram account, send a DM to the bot's Instagram account. Wait 5–10 seconds. You should receive an AI-written reply directly in your Instagram DMs.
Error: "OAuthException: Invalid OAuth access token" — your long-lived token is incorrect or expired. Go back to Lesson 1 and generate a new one.

Error: "The recipient is not subscribed to this app" — the Instagram account did not accept the tester invitation. On their phone: Instagram → Settings → Apps and Websites → Tester Invites → Accept.

Every DM sent to the Instagram Business account now receives an AI-written reply within seconds, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Key Takeaways — Lesson 2
Capture a real test DM first — the variable paths you see in the captured data are the exact paths to use in your modules.
The access token goes in the Query String as a URL parameter, not in the request Headers.
"messaging_type": "RESPONSE" is required in the body — without it Meta may reject the message send request.
If you get "recipient not subscribed" — the Instagram account must accept the tester invitation from the Instagram app.

Advanced Strategies + How to Sell the Instagram Package

With one extra feature — comment auto-reply — you can double your price from $500 to $1,000+. This lesson covers the upgrade, the exact target businesses, the outreach message that gets replies, and how to handle the #1 client objection.

Upgrade: Comment Auto-Reply (charge extra $200)
When someone comments "how much?" on a product photo, that comment sits unanswered and the customer often goes to a competitor. With comment auto-reply, the bot replies within seconds — keeping the customer engaged. Here is how to add it.
1
Subscribe to Instagram "comments" in your Facebook App
Go to your Facebook App → Webhooks in the left sidebar. Find the Instagram section. Click Edit next to your current subscription. In the list of fields, find comments and check its checkbox. Click Update. The same webhook URL now receives both DMs and comment events.
2
Add a Router in Make.com to separate DMs from Comments
In your Make.com scenario, click the + after the Webhook circle. In the app search type Router. Click Router — it is a built-in Make.com tool, not an external app. Two paths branch off from the router circle. Path 1 will handle DMs. Path 2 will handle comments.
On Path 1 (DMs): click the wrench icon on the connector line between Router and the first module → click Set up a filter → Condition: the webhook data contains the text messaging. Connect your existing OpenAI and HTTP modules to Path 1.

On Path 2 (Comments): add a new filter → Condition: the webhook data contains changes. Add new OpenAI and HTTP modules for comment replies on Path 2.
3
Configure the comment reply HTTP module
On Path 2, add an HTTP module to reply to the comment. Use this endpoint:
https://graph.facebook.com/v18.0/{COMMENT_ID}/replies
Replace {COMMENT_ID} with the variable from the webhook data that contains the comment ID — find it in the captured webhook data under entry[].changes[].value.id.

Method: POST
Query String: access_token = your Long-Lived Token
Body (JSON):
{"message": "{{openai_reply_variable}}"}
4
Add a keyword filter so the bot only replies to buying-intent comments
Not every comment needs a bot reply — only questions about price, availability, and ordering. Add a Filter on Path 2 before the OpenAI module. Click the wrench icon on the connector line. Set the condition: comment text Contains one of these keywords:
price, how much, cost, available, delivery, order, where, shipping, pret, cost, comanda, livrare, disponibil
This filter ensures the bot only activates when a comment contains a purchase-related question. Compliments like "beautiful!" are left for the business owner to handle personally — which is appropriate and keeps interactions feeling human.

Outreach: Find Prospects and Send the Perfect Message
The most effective Instagram bot prospect is a business that already posts regularly but has unanswered questions in their comments. Here is the exact outreach method.
1
Best businesses to target for Instagram bots
These businesses get high Instagram DM and comment volume and have strong ROI for automation:
Clothing boutiques — constant "what sizes do you have?" and "do you ship?" DMs
Fitness studios — class inquiries, pricing, availability
Beauty salons and lash studios — booking inquiries and price questions every hour
Food brands and restaurants — order inquiries, menu questions, delivery info
Handmade product sellers — custom order questions, shipping questions
2
Find unanswered comments on their posts before you message
Go to the prospect's Instagram profile. Click on their last 3–5 posts. Scroll through the comments. Look for questions that have zero reply — "How much is this?", "Do you deliver to [city]?", "Is this available in size M?". Write down the specific post and the specific comment text. This takes 3 minutes per prospect.
3
Send this outreach DM — use the specific unanswered comment as proof
Send this message from your Instagram account, customized with the real comment you found:
Instagram DM Outreach Template
Hi [Name], I was looking through your posts and noticed this comment on your [post description] from [X days] ago still hasn't been answered: "[exact comment text]" That's a potential customer who may have already bought from someone else. I build AI systems that automatically reply to every comment and DM within seconds — 24/7 in your brand voice. Want me to show you a 30-second demo of how it would work for your account?
This message is effective because it shows a specific, real problem — not a hypothetical. The prospect can verify the unanswered comment exists. It makes the value immediately obvious.
4
Handle the #1 objection: "I reply to my DMs myself"

Key point: position the bot as an assistant, not a replacement. The business owner still "owns" the conversation.
