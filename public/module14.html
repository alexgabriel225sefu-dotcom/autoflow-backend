Website · Lesson 1

Build Your Agency Website in One Day

You do not need a complex, expensive website. You need one page that clearly explains who you help, what you do, and how to contact you — with social proof and a clear call to action. This lesson builds it in one day using Carrd.co for free.

Why Your Website Matters (and What It Actually Does)
Your website is not your primary lead source — outreach is. But your website is what people check after they receive your outreach message or after a sales call. If your website looks professional, it confirms you are legitimate. If it looks bad or doesn't exist, you lose deals you otherwise would have won.
Your website needs to do exactly one thing: convince a skeptical business owner that you are a real, professional agency who has done this before. That's it.
Step 1 — Set Up Carrd.co (Free One-Page Website)
1
Go to carrd.co and sign up
Open carrd.co. Click Build a site →. Select Start from scratch or choose a clean minimal template. Create a free account with your email. The free plan allows one site with basic features — enough for your agency site.
2
Choose a dark, minimal template
Browse the templates. Choose one with a dark background and minimal design — this looks more premium and matches the AI/tech positioning. Avoid templates with bright colors or complex layouts.
Step 2 — The 6 Sections Every Agency Website Needs
1
Hero Section — What You Do and Who You Help
The headline must be specific. Not "AI automation agency" — instead:
Headline: "We Build AI Systems That Reply to Your Customers 24/7 — Automatically"

Subheadline: "WhatsApp bots, Instagram automation, and lead follow-up systems for restaurants, clinics, and local businesses. Setup in one week. Results from day one."

CTA Button: "Book a Free 15-Min Call" — links to your Calendly or a contact form
2
Problem Section — What Your Clients Struggle With
Three cards, each describing a pain point your target client has:
Card 1: "Losing leads to slow replies — customers message you and don't hear back for hours. By then, they've already called your competitor."

Card 2: "Paying for manual work — you pay staff to answer the same 10 questions every day. These answers could be automated in an afternoon."

Card 3: "Missing out after hours — 40% of customer inquiries come in outside business hours. Without automation, you miss them all."
3
Services Section — What You Build
Three service cards with icons, names, and one-line descriptions:
📱 WhatsApp AI Bot — "Replies to every customer message in seconds. 24/7. In your brand voice."

📸 Instagram DM Bot — "Handles every DM and comment automatically. Never miss a lead from social media."

📊 Lead Follow-Up System — "Every contact form submission gets an instant AI-written reply. More conversions, zero manual work."
4
Social Proof — Results and Testimonials
This is the most important section for building trust. Include:
— At least 2 testimonials from real clients (get these from Module 4's testimonial request system)
— 2–3 specific result stats: "Restaurant X received 340 automated replies in the first month" or "Clinic Y reduced missed calls by 80%"

If you have no testimonials yet: use your own agency as the case study. "I automated my own agency operations — cutting admin work from 3 hours/day to 20 minutes. The same systems I use are what I build for clients."
5
Pricing Section — Simple and Clear
Show 3 tiers. Keep it simple:
Starter — $400 setup + $150/month — WhatsApp bot or Instagram bot
Growth — $800 setup + $250/month — WhatsApp + Instagram + Lead follow-up
Full System — $1,500+ setup + $400/month — Everything + CRM + Weekly reports

Add a note: "Custom pricing for larger businesses. Book a call to discuss."
6
Contact Section — Make It Easy to Reach You
Include: a simple contact form (name, email, business type, what they need), your WhatsApp number (with a direct WhatsApp link), and your Calendly link for booking calls directly.
For Calendly: go to calendly.com → sign up free → create a 15-minute event type → share the link. Calendly handles scheduling automatically — clients pick a time that works for both of you without back-and-forth emails.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 1
Your website confirms legitimacy — it doesn't generate leads. Outreach generates leads.
One specific headline beats a generic one every time. Name exactly who you help and what you do.
Social proof is the most important section. Even one real testimonial changes everything.
Use Calendly so prospects can book calls without emailing you — reduces friction dramatically.
Growth · Lesson 2

Scale to $10K/Month — The Growth Playbook

Going from $3K to $10K/month requires three things: more clients, higher prices, and less time per client. This lesson gives you the exact playbook for each.

How to Raise Your Prices Without Losing Clients
Most agency owners are afraid to raise prices. Here is the reality: your first 5 clients were priced low to build experience and testimonials. Once you have 3+ testimonials and real results, you are a different product. Raise prices for all new clients — not existing ones.
1
Raise new client prices every 5 clients
Client 1–3: $300–$500 setup, $150–$200/month (learning + testimonials). Client 4–6: $500–$800 setup, $200–$300/month. Client 7–10: $800–$1,200 setup, $300–$400/month. Client 10+: $1,500+ setup, $400–$600/month. Your experience, testimonials and confidence justify every increase.
2
Upgrade existing clients to higher tiers
Every 3–4 months, offer your existing clients an upgrade. "I've been thinking about how to add more value to your automation. I can add Instagram automation to your current WhatsApp bot for $300 one-time and $100 extra per month — want me to show you how it would work?"
This upsell converts at 40–60% with happy clients because they already trust you. It is the highest ROI activity in your agency.
How to Get More Clients Faster at Scale
1
Referral system — your best client acquisition channel
Build a formal referral program. For every client a client refers: give the referring client one free month of service ($150–$400 value). This costs you very little and creates a viral loop. Every satisfied client becomes a salesperson for your agency.
Script: "Hi [Name], quick question — do you know any other business owners who might benefit from the same system you have? If someone you refer becomes a client, I'll give you your next month free. Just introduce us over WhatsApp."
2
Industry specialization — become THE expert
After your first 5–6 clients, pick one industry to dominate. If 3 of your 6 clients are restaurants — you are now a restaurant automation specialist. Update your website, your outreach, and your LinkedIn to reflect this. "I specifically help restaurants automate their customer communication" converts 3x better than "I help all businesses."
3
Content on LinkedIn and TikTok — inbound leads
Post one piece of content per week about AI automation for businesses. Share case studies (with client permission), before/after results, behind-the-scenes of what you build. This creates inbound leads — business owners who find you and reach out. At $10K/month, roughly 40% of clients should come from inbound.
The Math to $10K/Month
There are two paths to $10K/month. Choose based on your capacity:
A
Many smaller clients — 25 clients at $400/month average
Requires: strong systems, templates, make.com expertise to manage 25 clients in under 15 hours/week. Best for: people who enjoy variety and building relationships with many businesses.
B
Fewer larger clients — 6–8 clients at $1,200–$1,500/month
Requires: ability to build complex omnichannel systems (voice + WhatsApp + Instagram + CRM + reports). Higher skill level but much less operational complexity. Best for: people who prefer deep work with fewer relationships.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 2
Raise prices for new clients every 5 clients. Your experience and testimonials justify every increase.
A formal referral program (one free month per referral) creates organic growth with zero outreach cost.
Industry specialization converts 3x better than being a generalist once you have 5+ clients in one sector.
Choose your path: 25 clients at $400/month OR 8 clients at $1,300/month — both reach $10K.
Enterprise · Lesson 3

Landing Enterprise Clients — $5,000–$20,000 Projects

Enterprise clients — hotel chains, restaurant groups, medical networks, insurance companies — pay 10–50x what small businesses pay. The sales process is different, the deliverables are different, but the technology is the same. This lesson shows you how to access this market.

What Enterprise Clients Need That Small Businesses Don't
1
Formal proposal documents — not emails
Enterprise procurement requires a formal proposal document — a PDF with your agency branding, executive summary, technical overview, implementation timeline, pricing, and terms. Use Google Docs or Canva to create this. The AI Proposal Generator from Module 12 generates the content — you just format it as a PDF.
2
Service Level Agreement (SLA)
Enterprise clients require a written SLA — what uptime you guarantee, what response time you commit to for support issues, what happens if the system goes down. Use a simple one-page SLA template. Free templates available at Docracy.com or LegalTemplates.net.
3
Pilot project approach
Enterprise clients rarely sign large contracts immediately. Propose a paid pilot: "Let's start with a 30-day pilot at one location for $1,500. We implement the system, measure the results, and if you are satisfied we roll it out to all 15 locations at $800/location setup + $200/location/month." This reduces their risk and gets your foot in the door.
How to Find and Approach Enterprise Clients
1
LinkedIn — find Operations Directors and Digital Managers
Enterprise decisions are made by: Director of Operations, Head of Digital, Marketing Director, or COO — not the CEO. Search LinkedIn for these titles at companies with 50–500 employees. Connect and send a message focused on operational efficiency and ROI — not features.
Example LinkedIn message: "Hi [Name], I help hospitality groups automate their customer communication across all locations — reducing response time from hours to seconds and saving 20+ staff hours per week per location. Would a brief call make sense to see if this could work for [Company]?"
2
Industry trade events and associations
Join industry associations for your target sector — restaurant associations, hotel federations, medical practice groups. Attend their events. Sponsor their newsletter if affordable. Enterprise buyers trust people they have met in their industry context far more than cold outreach.
3
Case studies from your small business clients
Your best enterprise pitch tool is a case study from a similar small business. "We automated customer communication for a 3-location restaurant group in Bucharest — reducing missed inquiries by 85% and saving the equivalent of 2 full-time customer service positions. I can show you exactly how we would apply the same system to your 12 locations."
✦ The Enterprise Multiplier
One enterprise client at $500/location/month × 15 locations = $7,500/month from a single client relationship. The system you build is nearly identical to what you build for small businesses — you just deploy it more times and charge a management fee for the additional complexity. Build the small business client base first to develop your skills and testimonials, then use those to land one enterprise client that doubles your revenue.
