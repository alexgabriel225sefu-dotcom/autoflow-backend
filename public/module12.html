Strategy · Lesson 1

The Proposal System That Closes More Deals

Most freelancers lose deals not because their price is too high, but because their proposal arrives too late and reads too generic. This lesson shows you exactly what a winning proposal contains, what to never include, and why sending within 2 hours of the call is the single biggest lever you have.

Why Proposals Decide the Deal
The moment a sales call ends, the client's excitement starts to decay. Every hour that passes, they cool down, get distracted by other problems, or talk to a competitor. B2B sales data shows that proposals sent within 2 hours close at 3× the rate of proposals sent 24 hours later. The AI Proposal Generator means your proposal reaches them before that momentum dies — usually while they are still at their desk thinking about the call.
The second reason proposals fail: they are copy-paste templates that make the client feel like prospect #47. A personalized proposal that references the exact words the client used on the call creates an immediate "they really understood my problem" reaction. GPT-4o, fed with your call notes, produces that level of personalization automatically.
The Anatomy of a Winning Proposal
1
Cover Page — makes it feel like a real deliverable
Include the client's business name, their industry, your agency name, and the date. Even in an email proposal, a clear header section with this information signals professionalism. It tells the client: "this document was made for me, not copied from a drawer."
Example header line: "Proposal for: Bella Vista Restaurant Group | Industry: Food & Beverage | Prepared by: YourAgency.com | Date: [Today]"
2
Problem Summary — 2–3 sentences using their exact words
Write a short paragraph describing the problem they described to you on the call. Do not reinterpret or reframe — use their language. If they said "we miss messages on weekends," write exactly that. This is the single most important trust signal in the entire document. It proves you listened.
Bad: "You are experiencing communication challenges." Good: "Your team currently misses customer messages on weekends and after 6 PM, which you estimate is costing you 10–15 bookings per month. You also mentioned that your staff spend roughly 2 hours per day answering the same menu and reservation questions repeatedly."
3
Your Solution — specific automations in plain language
Describe exactly what you will build. Use plain English — no Make.com, no API, no webhook. The client does not care about the technology. They care about what it does. "I will build a WhatsApp bot that automatically replies to every customer message with your restaurant's hours, menu link, and reservation form — 24 hours a day, 7 days a week."
Never say: "I'll set up an OpenAI-powered webhook integration with a Make.com scenario." Say: "I'll build an AI assistant that handles all customer messages automatically in your brand voice."
4
What They Get — exact deliverables, not hours or effort
List bullet points of every deliverable. Not "10 hours of work" — instead: 1) WhatsApp bot live on your number, 2) Instagram DM auto-responder configured, 3) FAQ knowledge base with 30 questions loaded, 4) Monthly performance report, 5) 30-day support included. Deliverables are tangible. Hours feel like cost. Results feel like value.
5
Timeline — a specific promise
State exactly when the system will be live. "Your automation system will be fully operational within 7 days of receiving the setup fee." A specific timeline reduces perceived risk — the client knows this won't drag on for weeks with nothing to show.
6
Investment — a table, not a paragraph
Present pricing as a simple two-row table. Never bury the price in a paragraph — tables make it easy to read and look professional:
| Item | Price |
| One-time Setup Fee | $600 |
| Monthly Retainer | $250/month |

Add one sentence after the table: "Most clients recover this investment within the first month from leads that were previously lost to slow response times."
7
Next Steps — exactly what happens after yes
Make the path forward completely clear. "To proceed: reply to this email with 'Yes, let's start' and I will send an invoice for the setup fee. Once payment is received, I begin building immediately and will be in touch within 24 hours with onboarding details." The client should never have to wonder what to do next.
8
Guarantee — removes the remaining risk
Include one short guarantee statement: "If the automation system does not perform as described after delivery, I will fix it at no additional cost. No arguments, no exceptions." This single sentence removes the biggest remaining objection — what if it doesn't work? Almost no freelancer does this, which means it immediately differentiates you.
What NOT to Include
1
Technical details the client does not care about
Do not mention Make.com, OpenAI, GPT-4o, webhooks, API keys, JSON, or any technical infrastructure. If the client asks, you can explain verbally. In the proposal, the technology is invisible — what matters is the outcome. Technical details introduce doubt ("this sounds complicated, what if it breaks?") and distract from the value conversation.
2
Screenshots of Make.com or tool interfaces
Never put a Make.com scenario screenshot in a client proposal. It looks amateur and it raises questions about complexity and fragility. A client seeing your workflow diagram is not a confidence builder — it is a reason to hesitate.
3
Hourly breakdowns or "effort" descriptions
Do not say "This involves 8 hours of setup" or "I estimate 3 hours of monthly maintenance." Clients who see hourly thinking start calculating: "$300/month for 3 hours = $100/hour, that seems high." Instead, only present outputs and results. You are selling a system, not your time.
The Before & After Frame — the most powerful section
Add a "Before vs After" section to every proposal. Two short columns. Before: "Customer messages go unanswered on weekends. Staff spend 2 hours/day on repetitive questions. 10–15 bookings missed per month." After: "Every customer message gets an instant reply 24/7. Staff focus on hospitality, not admin. Zero missed booking inquiries." This is not about features — it is about contrast. Contrast creates desire.
Timing and Delivery
1
Send within 2 hours of the call
Fill in the Google Form immediately after hanging up — within 10 minutes while everything is fresh. The Make.com automation generates and sends the proposal in under 60 seconds. The client receives a professional proposal before they've even finished their next task. This speed signals competence and urgency.
2
Attach a PDF and include a personal note
The AI system generates the proposal in the email body. Optionally, copy it into a Google Doc, export as PDF, and attach it. Include a short personal line at the top of the email: "Hi [Name], great talking with you earlier — as promised, here's a full breakdown of what I'd build for [Business]. Let me know if you have questions." This 1-sentence personal note makes it feel human, not automated.
✦ The Proposal Mindset
Every sentence in a proposal should move the client toward saying yes. The Problem Summary proves you listened. The Solution builds confidence. The Results justify the price. The Guarantee removes risk. The Next Steps make it easy to act. Read every section and ask: "does this move them toward yes, or does it create doubt?" Cut anything that creates doubt.
✦ Exercise — Write Your Proposal Template
Before moving to Lesson 2, write a proposal for an imaginary restaurant client. Use this scenario: they told you they miss 15 messages per weekend, their staff spends 2 hours daily on FAQs, and they want a WhatsApp bot. Write each of the 8 sections above. Time yourself. Notice how long it takes manually — that is the time you are about to eliminate completely with the AI system.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 1
Proposals sent within 2 hours of a call close at 3× the rate of proposals sent the next day.
Use the client's exact words in the Problem Summary — this is the highest-trust moment in the document.
Never mention technical tools. Sell outcomes, not infrastructure.
The Before & After frame creates desire by making the contrast between their current situation and the solution visceral.
A guarantee removes the final objection. Almost no competitor offers one — so you win on trust.
Build · Lesson 2

Build the AI Proposal Generator in Make.com

The full flow: you fill a Google Form on your phone right after a call → Make.com triggers automatically → GPT-4o writes a professional, personalized proposal using all your notes → Gmail sends it to the client. Total time from form submission to proposal in client's inbox: under 60 seconds.

Part 1 — Create the Post-Call Google Form
1
Open Google Forms and start a new blank form
Go to forms.google.com in your browser. You see a grid of existing forms (or an empty screen if this is your first). Click the large + button in the bottom right corner — it is a colorful circle with a plus sign. A new blank form opens. You see an "Untitled form" heading at the top.
Click directly on "Untitled form" text to edit it. Type: Post-Call Notes — Proposal Generator. Press Enter. Click on "Form description" below and type: "Fill this in immediately after every sales call. Takes 3 minutes. Generates a professional proposal automatically."
2
Add field 1: Client Business Name
The form already has one blank question. Click on it. You see a text field with "Question" placeholder text — click it and type Client Business Name. On the right side of that question block, there is a dropdown that says "Multiple choice" — click it and select Short answer from the dropdown list. The question type changes to show a single-line text field below it.
Click the toggle on the right of the question to make it Required (the toggle turns blue/purple). Required fields display a red asterisk in the form — this ensures you never accidentally submit with a blank field.
3
Add field 2: Client Contact Name
Click the + button on the right-side floating toolbar (the toolbar with pencil, image, and video icons floats to the right of each question block). A new question appears below. Click the question text and type Client Contact Name. Set type to Short answer. Toggle Required on.
4
Add field 3: Client Email
Click + again. Type Client Email. Set type to Short answer. Toggle Required on. Then click the three-dot menu (...) at the bottom right of that question → click Response validation. A new section appears — set it to Text → Email. This makes Google Forms reject responses that are not valid email addresses — preventing typos.
5
Add field 4: Industry / Type of Business
Click +. Type Industry / Type of Business. Set type to Short answer. Required on. Example answers you will type: Restaurant, Real Estate Agency, Beauty Salon, Dental Clinic, E-commerce Store.
6
Add field 5: Main Problem They Have
Click +. Type Main Problem They Have (use their exact words). Set type to Paragraph — this gives a multi-line text area. Required on. This field is the most important input — the more detail you add here, the more personalized GPT-4o's proposal becomes.
7
Add field 6: Automations to Build
Click +. Type Automations to Build (list what you discussed). Set type to Paragraph. Required on. You will type things like: "WhatsApp bot for customer FAQs, Instagram DM auto-responder, lead follow-up email sequence after form submission."
8
Add fields 7, 8, and 9: Pricing and Timeline
Add three more Short answer fields: Setup Fee (e.g. $600), Monthly Retainer (e.g. $250/month), and Delivery Timeline in Days (e.g. 7). Make all three Required.
9
Link form responses to Google Sheets
At the top of the form editor, click the Responses tab (next to "Questions"). You see a summary screen that says "0 responses." Look for a green Google Sheets icon on the right side of the screen — click it. A dialog appears: select Create a new spreadsheet. Name it Post-Call Notes Responses. Click Create.
Google Sheets opens automatically. You see one header row with a column for each form field and a "Timestamp" column on the left. Every form submission will now appear as a new row in this sheet. Bookmark this sheet URL — you will need it in Make.com.
Part 2 — Build the Make.com Scenario
10
Open Make.com and create a new Scenario
Go to make.com and log in. In the left sidebar, click Scenarios. In the top right, click the blue + Create a new scenario button. A blank canvas appears — a dark background with a large circle in the center that says "Choose a starting app or service."
11
Add the Google Sheets trigger
Click the large circle. A search box appears. Type Google Sheets. Click the Google Sheets logo when it appears. A list of triggers and actions appears. Scroll down to find Watch New Rows and click it. A configuration panel slides in from the right.
Click Add under Connection to connect your Google account. A browser popup opens — sign in and click Allow. Back in Make.com, the connection appears. Now configure: Spreadsheet ID → click the field → select Post-Call Notes Responses. Sheet Name → select Form Responses 1 (the default sheet name). Limit → type 1. Click OK.
12
Add Module 2: OpenAI Create a Chat Completion
Click the + icon that appears to the right of the Google Sheets bubble. Search for OpenAI. Click the OpenAI logo. Select Create a Chat Completion. Connect your OpenAI account (you need an API key from platform.openai.com → API Keys → Create new secret key).
Model: select gpt-4o from the dropdown. Under Messages, click Add item. Set Role to system. In Content, type: "You are a professional B2B proposal writer. Write formal, concise, persuasive proposals for an AI automation agency. Use the client's exact words when describing their problem. Never use placeholder text."
13
Add the user message with the full prompt template
Click Add item again. Set Role to user. In Content, paste this entire prompt — then click each {{variable}} placeholder and replace it with the matching column from your Google Sheets row (Make.com shows a list of available variables when you click inside the field):
Write a complete, professional proposal email for an AI automation agency client. CLIENT INFORMATION: - Business Name: [Google Sheets: Client Business Name] - Contact Name: [Google Sheets: Client Contact Name] - Client Email: [Google Sheets: Client Email] - Industry: [Google Sheets: Industry / Type of Business] - Their Problem (use these exact words): [Google Sheets: Main Problem They Have] - Automations Requested: [Google Sheets: Automations to Build] - Setup Fee: [Google Sheets: Setup Fee] - Monthly Retainer: [Google Sheets: Monthly Retainer] - Delivery Timeline: [Google Sheets: Delivery Timeline in Days] days PROPOSAL STRUCTURE — write each section in order: 1. SUBJECT LINE (write "SUBJECT:" on its own line first) 2. Greeting using their first name 3. Problem Summary: 2-3 sentences describing their specific problem using their exact words above 4. Before vs After: brief contrast of their current situation vs after the automation 5. Proposed Solution: specific automations listed, in plain business language (no technical jargon) 6. What They Get: 4-5 bullet point deliverables 7. Timeline: when the system will be fully live 8. Investment: setup fee and monthly retainer in a clear table format 9. Guarantee statement: one sentence promising to fix any issues at no cost 10. Next Steps: exactly what to do to proceed 11. Professional sign-off TONE: Professional, warm, confident. Not corporate or robotic. LENGTH: 350-450 words total (not counting the subject line). CRITICAL: Do NOT use placeholder text like [Name] or [Business]. Use the real values provided above.
14
Add Module 3: Text Parser to extract the subject line
Click + after the OpenAI module. Search for Text Parser. Click it. Select Match Pattern. In the Pattern field type exactly:
SUBJECT: (.+)
In the Text field, click and select the Choices: Content variable from the OpenAI module (this is the full GPT-4o output). Set Global to No. Click OK. This module extracts just the subject line text — everything after "SUBJECT: " on the first line — into a separate variable you can use as the email subject.
15
Add Module 4: Gmail — Send an Email
Click + after Text Parser. Search for Gmail. Click it. Select Send an Email. Connect your Google/Gmail account. Configure the fields:
To: click the field → select Client Email from Google Sheets variables
Subject: click → select the $[1] Match variable from the Text Parser (the extracted subject line)
Content: click → select Choices: Content from OpenAI module
Content Type: select HTML so any formatting renders correctly

Click OK.
16
Enable scheduling and save the Scenario
At the bottom of the Make.com canvas, find the Scheduling toggle — it is off by default (grey). Click it to turn it ON (it turns blue). A scheduling dialog appears — set it to check every 15 minutes. Click OK. Then click Save (the floppy disk icon in the bottom left of the canvas).
17
Test the complete flow end to end
Go back to your Google Form. Click the eye icon (Preview) at the top right to open the live form. Fill in every field with real-ish test data — use your own email address as the client email so you receive the test proposal. Click Submit.
Back in Make.com, click Run once (bottom left of canvas). Watch each module turn green one by one: Google Sheets finds the new row, OpenAI generates the proposal, Text Parser extracts the subject, Gmail sends the email. Check your inbox. The proposal should arrive within 30 seconds. Read it carefully — does it sound professional? Does it use the test information you entered? Does the subject line make sense?
✓ What Success Looks Like
After every sales call, you spend 3 minutes filling the form on your phone. Within 60 seconds, the client receives a polished, personalized proposal that references their specific situation. You are now the most responsive, professional agency they have encountered — and it cost you 3 minutes. Competitors are still writing theirs manually 24 hours later.
✦ Exercise — Send Yourself 3 Test Proposals
Submit the form 3 times with 3 different fictional businesses — a restaurant, a real estate agency, and a dental clinic. Each time, use different problems and different prices. Read all 3 proposals. Are they distinct and personalized, or do they read the same? Adjust the system prompt or user message prompt until every proposal feels genuinely tailored to that specific client.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 2
Fill the form within 10 minutes of the call — while the client's words are still fresh in your memory.
The more detail you put in "Main Problem They Have," the more specific and persuasive GPT-4o's proposal becomes.
The Text Parser module is essential — without it, the full proposal text becomes the email subject line.
Always test with your own email first. Never let a system send to real clients before you have personally verified the output is excellent.
Sales · Lesson 3

Track Proposals and Follow Up Automatically

Sending the proposal is step one. Most deals are won on the follow-up — 50% of sales happen after the 5th contact, yet most agency owners follow up once (if at all). This lesson adds an automated Proposal Tracker and two follow-up emails that run without you remembering to do a single thing.

Part 1 — Add the Proposal Tracker to Your CRM Sheet
1
Open your main CRM Google Sheet
Go to sheets.google.com and open the Client CRM spreadsheet you set up in Module 8. You see your existing tabs at the bottom of the screen (Clients, Leads, etc.).
2
Add a new tab called "Proposals"
At the very bottom left of the screen, next to your existing sheet tabs, click the + icon (it is a small grey plus sign). A new blank sheet tab appears, initially named "Sheet2" or similar. Double-click the tab name and type Proposals. Press Enter.
3
Create the column headers in row 1
Click cell A1 and type the following headers, pressing Tab to move to the next column:
A1: Date Sent | B1: Client Name | C1: Business Name | D1: Client Email | E1: Setup Fee | F1: Monthly | G1: Status | H1: Follow-Up 1 Date | I1: Follow-Up 2 Date | J1: Notes

Select row 1 (click the "1" row number on the left). Make it bold: Ctrl+B (Windows) or Cmd+B (Mac). Change the background color to dark grey using the paint bucket icon in the toolbar. Change text color to white.
4
Add data validation to the Status column
Click cell G2. Hold Shift and click G500 — this selects the entire Status column for future rows. In the top menu, click Data → Data validation. In the panel on the right, click + Add rule. Set Criteria to Dropdown. Add these options one by one: Sent, Followed Up 1, Followed Up 2, Won, Lost. Click Done. Now the Status column only accepts these values — no typos or inconsistencies.
5
Update the Make.com proposal scenario to log each proposal
Go back to your Make.com proposal scenario. Click the last module (Gmail Send an Email). Click + to add a module after it. Search for Google Sheets. Select Add a Row.
Spreadsheet ID → your CRM sheet. Sheet Name → Proposals. Map the columns:
A (Date Sent) → now() or the "Current Date" Make.com variable
B (Client Name) → Google Sheets: Client Contact Name
C (Business Name) → Google Sheets: Client Business Name
D (Client Email) → Google Sheets: Client Email
E (Setup Fee) → Google Sheets: Setup Fee
F (Monthly) → Google Sheets: Monthly Retainer
G (Status) → type the text Sent (hardcoded)

Click OK. Save the scenario. Now every time a proposal is sent, it is automatically logged in the tracker.
Part 2 — Automated Day-2 Follow-Up Email
6
Create a new Make.com Scenario for follow-ups
In Make.com, click Scenarios in the sidebar. Click + Create a new scenario. Name this one Proposal Follow-Up — Day 2.
7
Set the trigger to run daily at 9 AM
Click the large starting circle. Search for Schedule. Select Every Day. Set the time to 09:00. This scenario checks your proposals sheet every morning at 9 AM to find any that need a Day-2 follow-up.
8
Add Google Sheets — Search Rows
Click +. Search for Google Sheets. Select Search Rows. Connect your CRM sheet → Proposals tab. In Filter, add two conditions:
Filter 1: Column G (Status) — Text equals — Sent
Filter 2: Column A (Date Sent) — Date — is between — 2 days ago and 1 day ago

This finds every proposal that was sent exactly 2 days ago and still has Status = Sent (meaning no reply yet).
9
Add OpenAI to generate the follow-up email
Click +. Add OpenAI → Create a Chat Completion → gpt-4o. System message: "You write short, genuine, human-sounding follow-up emails. No corporate language." User message:
Write a short follow-up email for a proposal sent 2 days ago with no reply. Client name: [Column B from Search Rows] Business: [Column C from Search Rows] Setup fee quoted: [Column E from Search Rows] The email must be: - Maximum 80 words - Mention that you sent a proposal a couple of days ago - Create gentle urgency: you have limited availability this month - End with a simple yes/no question: "Is this still something you're interested in exploring?" - Sound like a real person writing a real email, not a sales template
10
Send the follow-up via Gmail
Click +. Add Gmail → Send an Email. To: Column D (Client Email) from Search Rows. Subject: type Re: Your automation proposal — then click and insert Column C (Business Name). Content: OpenAI output. Click OK.
11
Update the Proposals sheet to mark Follow-Up 1 sent
Click +. Add Google Sheets → Update a Row. Sheet: Proposals. Row number: the row number from the Search Rows result. Set: Column G (Status) → Followed Up 1. Column H (Follow-Up 1 Date) → today's date. Click OK. This prevents this same client from getting another Day-2 email tomorrow.
Part 3 — Automated Day-5 Follow-Up Email
12
Create a third Make.com Scenario: Day-5 Follow-Up
Create another new scenario. Name it Proposal Follow-Up — Day 5. Same daily schedule trigger at 9 AM. Same Google Sheets → Search Rows module, but this time filter: Column G = Followed Up 1 AND Column H (Follow-Up 1 Date) is between 3 and 4 days ago.
13
The Day-5 follow-up prompt — different angle, more direct
Add OpenAI with this prompt:
Write a final 60-word follow-up email for a proposal sent 5 days ago with no response. Client: [Client Name] Business: [Business Name] The email should: - Ask directly if they are still interested or if the timing is not right - Be completely pressure-free — "no worries either way" - Offer to reconnect in 3 months if now is not the right time - Sound genuine, human, and respectful of their time - NOT mention the specific price or proposal details again
14
Send and update the status to Followed Up 2
Add Gmail → Send an Email (same To and Subject as Day-2 follow-up). Then add Google Sheets → Update a Row: set Column G to Followed Up 2 and Column I (Follow-Up 2 Date) to today. After this, no more automated emails — the deal either moves forward manually or goes to Lost.
The Follow-Up Email Templates
Day-2 Follow-Up Email Example
Subject: Re: Your automation proposal — Bella Vista

Hi Marco,

Just following up on the proposal I sent over a couple of days ago for your WhatsApp automation system. I wanted to make sure it landed in your inbox and not your spam folder.

I have one setup slot available this month — happy to hold it if you're still interested. Is this something you'd like to move forward with?

Best,
Your Name
Short, friendly, no pressure, ends with a clear yes/no question.
Day-5 Final Follow-Up Email Example
Subject: Re: Your automation proposal — Bella Vista

Hi Marco,

One final note — totally understand if the timing isn't right or priorities have shifted. No pressure at all.

If you'd like to revisit this in a few months, just reach out and I'll be happy to pick up where we left off. Wishing you and the restaurant all the best.

Your Name
This "closing the loop" email often gets a reply when the two earlier ones did not.
Tracking Your Proposal Conversion Rate
At the end of each month, count: Won ÷ Total Proposals Sent × 100 = your Proposal Conversion Rate. Industry average for AI automation agencies is 20–30%. If yours is below 15%, the problem is the proposal content — run more test proposals and refine the GPT-4o prompt. If yours is above 30%, you are targeting the right clients and your follow-up system is working. Do not change anything.
✦ The Revenue Impact
Most agency owners manually follow up on fewer than 30% of sent proposals — they forget, feel awkward, or run out of time. With this system, 100% of proposals receive two follow-ups, on schedule, automatically. Industry data shows 50% of sales occur after the 5th contact. Most people give up after 1. Automated follow-up alone has been shown to double closing rates for service agencies.

Key Takeaways — Lesson 3
100% follow-up rate on every proposal is only possible with automation — manual follow-up always has gaps.
Always update the Status column after each follow-up — this is what prevents duplicate emails and keeps the system clean.
Day-2: create gentle urgency. Day-5: ask directly and give them a graceful exit — this often triggers the reply.
Track your conversion rate monthly. Below 15% = fix the proposal. Above 30% = raise your prices.
