Prospecting · Lesson 1

Finding Businesses That Need You Right Now

The best prospects are businesses that are already showing pain signals — they are already losing clients because they cannot respond fast enough. This lesson teaches you exactly how to find those businesses using Google Maps, Instagram, and a few minutes of research per prospect.

The "Pain Signal" Framework — 3 Signs a Business Desperately Needs You
Before you start searching randomly, know what you are looking for. These three signals tell you a business is losing clients right now due to slow or no responses — and that your automation is the exact solution they need.
1
Pain Signal: They reply to Google reviews saying "Sorry for the slow response"
Go to any business's Google Maps listing and read their reviews and the business's replies. If you see the business owner replying with phrases like "Sorry for the delayed response," "Apologies we didn't get back to you sooner," or "We're working on improving our response times" — this is a direct confession that they have a response problem.
How to find this: Go to maps.google.com → search for businesses in your niche → click on each one → scroll down to the "Reviews" section → read both the reviews AND the business's replies. This takes about 60 seconds per business and gives you extremely valuable intelligence for your outreach message.
2
Pain Signal: Their WhatsApp status says "I'll reply when I can"
Some business owners put status messages on their WhatsApp like "Busy — will reply later," "Away from phone," or "I'll get back to you ASAP." This is a business owner who is overwhelmed by messages and literally telling customers they will have to wait. That wait costs them clients.
How to check: If the business lists a WhatsApp number on their Google Maps or website, open WhatsApp → tap the new chat icon → type in their number → look at their profile. You can often see their status without sending a message. If you see a "busy" or "away" status, add them immediately to your priority prospect list.
3
Pain Signal: Instagram comments full of unanswered questions
Go to a business's Instagram profile. Tap their most recent 3 posts and read the comments. Look specifically for customer questions like "What are the prices?", "Do you deliver to [area]?", "Are you open on Sunday?", "How do I book?" that have NOT received a reply from the business account.
This is your strongest signal because it is public evidence. Every unanswered comment is a potential client who asked a question and either waited or went to a competitor. When you reach out to this business, you can reference exactly what you saw — which makes your message incredibly specific and hard to ignore.
Method 1 — Google Maps (Start Here, Takes 45 Minutes)
1
Open Google Maps in your browser
On your computer, open a new browser tab and go to maps.google.com. You will see a map of your current location with a search bar at the top left. If you are on your phone, open the Google Maps app — the icon looks like a red pin on a white background.
Make sure you are signed into your Google account. You do not need to be signed in to search, but being signed in lets you save places and access your search history later.
2
Type your niche and city into the search bar
Click inside the white search bar at the top left. Type your industry and your target city together. Examples of what to type: restaurants Bucharest or beauty salon Cluj-Napoca or real estate agency Timisoara. Press the Enter key on your keyboard.
The map will zoom to your city and fill with red pins — each pin is one business in that category. On the left side of the screen, a vertical list of businesses appears with their names, star ratings, address, and brief description. You might see "1–20 of 500+ results" — there are far more businesses than you need. Start with the first page of results.
3
Click on the first business to open its full profile
In the list on the left, click on the name of the first business. A panel slides open showing: the business name in large bold text at the top, their star rating and total review count (example: "4.3 ★ (187 reviews)"), their full address, phone number, website, and operating hours.
Look at the top right of this panel — you might see buttons like "Website," "Directions," "Call," and sometimes a blue WhatsApp icon. If you see the WhatsApp icon, this business uses WhatsApp — write "Yes" in your WhatsApp column on your prospect spreadsheet.
4
Apply the 4-point quality check before adding to your list
Before adding this business to your prospect list, check all 4 of these criteria. If they fail 2 or more, skip to the next business.
Check 1 — Review count: Do they have 50 or more reviews? Less than 50 usually means they are too small or too new to have a real budget.

Check 2 — Phone number visible: Is a phone number listed? If not, they may be very hard to contact.

Check 3 — Not permanently closed: Look under the business name — does it say "Permanently closed" or "Temporarily closed"? Skip those.

Check 4 — Active presence: Do they have a website link or Instagram mentioned in their profile? This signals they care about digital presence.
5
Scroll down to read their reviews — find the pain signals
While still viewing this business's profile, scroll down past their photos until you see the "Reviews" section. Read the first 5–10 reviews AND read any replies the business has posted. Look for the pain signal: the business apologizing for slow responses or not replying to negative reviews at all.
If a business has gotten negative reviews about slow response times and has not replied, that is even more significant — they are either too overwhelmed to respond or not monitoring their reviews. Both are signs they need help. Write this specific observation in the Notes column of your spreadsheet — you will reference it in your outreach message.
6
Add the qualifying business to your Google Sheet
Switch to your prospect tracking Google Sheet. In the next empty row, fill in the columns: Business Name (copy from Google Maps), Type (restaurant/salon/etc.), City, Phone (copy the phone number exactly as shown), and Website (copy the URL). Leave Instagram blank for now — you will find that in the next step.
In the Notes column, write a one-sentence observation about their specific situation. Example: "Restaurant with 180 reviews — 3 negative reviews mention slow WhatsApp response, owner apologized in reply." This note becomes the foundation of your outreach hook.
7
Find their Instagram account and check for pain signals
Open Instagram in a new tab. Click the search icon (magnifying glass icon at the bottom of the app, or the search bar at the top of the website). Type the business name. Look for their account in the results — usually the one with the most followers and a professional profile photo.
On their profile, tap their most recent post. Read the comments. Look for unanswered customer questions. If you find any, go back to your spreadsheet and update the Notes column: "4 unanswered DM questions in comments — people asking about prices and hours." Then write "Yes" or "No" in the Instagram column and add their @handle. Go back to Google Maps and click the next business. Repeat steps 3–7 until you have 20 businesses.
Method 2 — Instagram Hashtag Search (Great for Beauty, Fitness, Coaches)
1
Open Instagram and tap the Search icon
Open Instagram on your phone. At the bottom of the screen you see 5 icons: Home (house), Search (magnifying glass), Add (plus), Reels (play button), and Profile (person icon). Tap the Search icon (magnifying glass). The search screen opens with a search bar at the top and suggested content below.
2
Search a niche + city hashtag combination
Tap inside the search bar. Type a hashtag combining your niche and city. Examples: #restaurantbucharest, #salonnailsbucharest, #realestateromana, #fitnessbucharest. Tap the hashtag result (the one with the # symbol in front) — not the account result.
You will see a grid of posts from businesses and individuals who tagged this hashtag. Scroll through to find posts that look like they come from business accounts (promotional photos of food, services, properties, etc.) rather than personal accounts (people's vacation photos, etc.).
3
Tap a business-looking post and visit their profile
Tap on a post that looks like it is from a business. The post opens. Look at the account name at the top left of the post. Does it look like a business name? Tap the account name or their profile photo in the top left corner. This opens their full profile page.
On their profile page, check: follower count (500+ = established business), post count (10+ = active), bio section (do they mention services, location, phone, or a link?). Look for the Email or Call or Message action buttons directly under their bio — those appear on business accounts.
4
Run the "response test" — the fastest way to qualify any prospect
The response test is simple: look at their last 3 posts and count how many customer questions in the comments went unanswered. If a post has 10 comments and 4 of them are customer questions with no reply from the business — that business fails to respond to 40% of customer inquiries. They need you urgently.
Screenshot any posts with unanswered questions — you will reference these in your outreach message. Having a screenshot means you can say "I saw on your last post that three people asked about pricing and didn't receive a reply" — an observation that specific is nearly impossible to ignore.
Setting Up Your Prospect Spreadsheet — Complete Template
1
Open Google Sheets and create a new spreadsheet
Go to sheets.google.com in your browser. You will see a green and white page with a grid of recent documents and templates. At the top of the page, click the large white square with a multicolored plus sign — this creates a new blank spreadsheet. The spreadsheet opens with rows and columns (A, B, C... across the top, 1, 2, 3... down the side).
Click on the cell A1 (the very first cell in the top left corner). It will have a blue border around it when selected. Start typing to set up your column headers. Click each cell and type the header name, then press Tab to move to the next column.
2
Add the 10 column headers exactly as shown
Click cell A1 and type: Business Name. Press Tab. In B1 type: Niche. Press Tab. C1: Phone Number. D1: Instagram. E1: WhatsApp?. F1: Website. G1: Pain Signal Noted. H1: Date Contacted. I1: Platform Used. J1: Status.
To make the header row bold: click on the row number "1" on the far left (this selects the entire row). Then press Ctrl+B (on Windows) or Cmd+B (on Mac). The text in all header cells turns bold. You can also click the B button in the toolbar at the top. Then use Format → Alternating Colors to make the spreadsheet easier to read.
3
Set up the Status column dropdown menu
Click on cell J2. Hold Shift and click J21 (this selects 20 rows in the Status column). Go to the menu bar at the top and click Data. In the dropdown, click Data Validation. A panel opens on the right side. Under "Criteria," select "Dropdown" from the dropdown menu. In the items field, type these options on separate lines: Not Contacted, Contacted, Replied, Call Booked, Closed, Not Interested. Click Done.
Now when you click any cell in the Status column, a small dropdown arrow appears. Click the arrow and you can select from your predefined options instead of typing. This makes your tracking much faster and consistent. Your target: 20 prospects in this sheet before you send a single message.
The Golden Signal — Find the 20 Best Prospects in Your City
The single most powerful prospecting signal: go to Google Maps, search your niche, click on businesses, and read their reviews. Any business where the owner replied to a negative review with "Sorry for the slow response" or "Apologies we didn't get back to you" is already aware of their problem and predisposed to buy your solution. These businesses are your absolute priority prospects.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 1
The 3 pain signals tell you a business needs you right now: slow review responses, overwhelmed WhatsApp status, unanswered Instagram comments.
Google Maps takes 45 minutes to build a list of 20 qualified prospects — use it first before any other method.
Instagram hashtag search finds businesses you would never find on Maps — especially coaches, beauty, and fitness.
Build 20 prospects in your spreadsheet BEFORE sending any messages. Researching first makes your outreach dramatically more effective.

Outreach · Lesson 2

The Outreach Message That Gets Replies

Most outreach fails not because the offer is bad, but because the message is generic. Business owners receive dozens of sales messages every week and ignore almost all of them. This lesson gives you the exact formula for messages that get replies — plus complete copy-paste templates for WhatsApp, Instagram DM, and email.

Why Most Outreach Gets Ignored — The 3 Fatal Mistakes
X
Fatal Mistake 1: Talking about yourself instead of their problem
Most outreach starts with "Hi, I am [name] and I offer AI automation services..." The business owner immediately switches off. They do not care who you are — they care about their own problems. Start with them, not you.
X
Fatal Mistake 2: Being vague about what you actually do
Saying "I provide AI solutions for businesses" means nothing. Business owners cannot picture it, cannot evaluate it, and cannot decide if they want it. Be specific: "I build a system that automatically replies to every WhatsApp message your restaurant receives — even at 3am."
X
Fatal Mistake 3: Asking for too much too soon
Asking a stranger to buy something in the first message never works. The ask should be tiny — a 15-minute call, a quick demo, a simple question. Reduce the friction of saying yes to almost zero.
The 3-Line Formula That Works — Observation + Problem + Offer
Every effective outreach message has exactly three components. The entire message should be short enough to read in 20 seconds. Here is the formula:
1
Line 1 — A specific observation about THEIR business
Start with something specific you noticed about their actual business. Reference their review count, something you saw on their Instagram, a specific comment you read. This immediately proves you are not sending a mass message — you looked at them specifically.
Good examples: "I noticed your restaurant has 280 Google reviews — impressive for your area." / "I was looking at your last Instagram post and saw 4 customers asking about prices with no reply yet." / "I saw on your Google Maps profile that you replied to a review last week apologizing for slow response times."
2
Line 2 — The problem this creates, with a specific consequence
Name the exact problem that observation reveals, and tell them what it costs them. Be concrete — mention clients lost, time wasted, or money left on the table. People act when they feel the consequence of a problem, not when they hear abstract statistics.
Good examples: "That probably means you're losing booking inquiries every day to competitors who reply faster." / "Every unanswered DM is a potential client who messaged and then went somewhere else." / "That's probably costing you 5–10 potential clients per month in lost inquiries."
3
Line 3 — A specific offer with zero commitment required
Describe what you do in one sentence focused on the result they get (not the technology). Then ask for a tiny commitment — a 15-minute call or a quick demo. Make it feel effortless to say yes.
Good examples: "I build AI systems that reply to every message automatically — in seconds, 24/7. Would 15 minutes this week make sense to show you how it would work for [their business name]?" / "I could set this up for your restaurant so no message goes unanswered again. Want me to send you a quick demo so you can see it before we talk?"
Complete Template 1 — WhatsApp Message for Restaurants
Send this to restaurant owners via WhatsApp. Replace every [bracket] with real information from your research:
WhatsApp — Restaurant Owner
Hi [Owner's first name], I noticed [Restaurant Name] has [X] Google reviews — impressive for a restaurant in [City/Area]. I'm guessing you get a lot of WhatsApp and Instagram messages every day asking about your menu, hours, and reservations — and it's probably impossible to reply to all of them quickly enough. That likely means you're losing bookings to restaurants that reply instantly. I build AI systems that reply to every customer message within seconds — 24/7, even at 3am — in a voice that matches your restaurant's style. Your team never has to answer the same question twice. Would a 15-minute call this week make sense so I can show you exactly how it works?
Important: do not add more to this message. Do not make it longer. Short messages get 3x more replies than long ones. The goal is one reply — not to close the sale in the first message.
Complete Template 2 — Instagram DM for Any Business
Use this when sending a direct message on Instagram. Keep the tone slightly more casual since Instagram is a more informal platform:
Instagram DM — Any Business
Hi [Name] 👋 I was looking at your profile and noticed you get a lot of comments and DMs with questions — but quite a few of them seem to go unanswered (I counted [X] on your last post alone). Every unanswered message is a potential client who just moved on to a competitor who replied first. I build AI systems that reply instantly to every DM and comment automatically — 24/7, in your exact brand voice. Takes about a week to set up. Would you be open to a 15-min call this week? I can show you exactly what it would look like for [their business name].
If they have DMs restricted to "followers only," follow their account first and wait 24 hours before sending. This gets your message into their main inbox instead of the filtered Message Requests folder.
Complete Template 3 — Email Outreach (Formal, for Real Estate / Clinics)
Email — Subject: Quick question about [Business Name]
Hi [Name], I came across [Business Name] on Google and noticed you have [X] reviews — clearly you're doing something right. Quick question: how are you currently handling customer inquiries that come in outside business hours — WhatsApp messages, Instagram DMs, contact form submissions? I ask because most [restaurants/agencies/clinics] in your area lose 20–40% of potential clients simply because they can't reply fast enough. The business that replies first typically wins the client. I build AI systems that reply to every inquiry within seconds — personalized to your business, 24/7 — for a fraction of the cost of hiring an extra person. Would it be worth a 15-minute call this week to show you exactly how it works? [Your Name] [Your Phone]
Subject line tip: "Quick question about [Business Name]" works because it is personal and creates curiosity. Do not use generic subjects like "AI services" or "Partnership opportunity" — those get sent to spam.
The Follow-Up Sequence — What to Send If They Don't Reply
1
Day 1 — Send your initial outreach message
Send your first message using one of the templates above. Write the date and time you sent it in your prospect spreadsheet. Set the Status to "Contacted." Now wait 3 days before following up.
Most business owners are busy and check messages at irregular times. A 3-day wait is respectful and gives them enough time to have genuinely missed your message rather than deliberately ignored it.
2
Day 3 — Send one follow-up message
If no reply after 3 days, send this exact follow-up (short, casual, no pressure):
"Hi [Name], just following up on my message from [day]. Totally fine if the timing isn't right — just didn't want it to get lost. Happy to send you a 60-second demo of what I've built for similar businesses if that would make it easier to see what I mean."

This follow-up offers something new (a demo) which gives them a new reason to reply.
3
Day 7 — Final message, then move on
If still no reply after day 7, send one final message:
"Hi [Name], last message from me. I know you're busy so I'll leave it here. If you ever want to see how AI automation could save your team time on customer replies, just let me know — happy to help."

After this, mark the status as "No Response" and move to the next prospect. Never send more than 3 messages total — it becomes spam and harms your reputation.
How to Handle Common Replies Before the Call
Q
They ask: "How much does it cost?" before seeing any demo
Do not quote a price in the message. Pricing without context leads to "too expensive" reflexes. Instead reply:
"Great question — it depends on what you need exactly, so I don't want to give you a number that's off. That's actually what the 15-minute call is for — I'll understand your situation and give you an exact quote. Most businesses I work with pay between $150 and $300/month. Can we find 15 minutes this week?"
Q
They say: "Not interested" or "We're fine for now"
Respond with one low-pressure reframe and then respect their answer:
"Totally understand! If anything changes — or if you ever find yourself overwhelmed with messages — feel free to reach out. I work with a lot of [restaurants/salons/agencies] in [city] so I'm around if you need it."

Mark their status as "Not Interested" and move on. Do not push further. A respectful exit often leads to them reaching back out weeks later when they feel the pain more acutely.
The Daily Outreach Routine — The Only Number That Matters
Send a minimum of 5 personalized outreach messages every day. This is your non-negotiable daily target. 5 messages per day × 20 working days = 100 messages per month. At a 10% reply rate = 10 conversations. From 10 conversations, you typically close 1–2 clients. That is the math of this business.
Track every message in your Google Sheet. The moment you stop tracking, you lose momentum. After sending each message, update the Date Contacted and Status columns in your spreadsheet. This takes 30 seconds per prospect and gives you a clear picture of your pipeline every day.
What NOT to Do
Do not lead with price. Do not say "cheap" or "affordable." Do not send the exact same message to 50 businesses without personalizing anything — the reply rate drops to near zero. Do not call yourself a freelancer — say "I run an automation agency" or "I build AI systems." Do not ask for a 30-minute or 1-hour meeting — always ask for 15 minutes. Do not send more than 3 messages to the same person. Do not get discouraged by no-replies — they are normal and expected. Play the volume game patiently.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 2
Every outreach message must start with a specific observation about THEIR business — never about you.
Use the 3-line formula: Observation + Problem with consequence + Specific offer with tiny commitment.
Send 5 personalized messages per day minimum. Track every single one in your Google Sheet.
Maximum 3 messages per prospect (Day 1, Day 3, Day 7). After that, move on — never spam.

Then send all 5 messages today. Write the date in the Date Contacted column for each one.
Sales · Lesson 3

Closing the Deal & Setting Your Price

A business owner replied and wants to talk. You have a 25-minute call to understand their problem, show them a demo, and close the deal. This lesson gives you the exact call structure — what to say in each minute, what to do when they object, and how to collect payment.

Before the Call — What to Do in the 5 Minutes Before It Starts
1
Open their Google Maps listing, website, and Instagram in separate tabs
Before you join the call, spend 5 minutes doing a quick review. Open 3 browser tabs: (1) their Google Maps listing, (2) their website or Facebook page, (3) their Instagram profile. Read their recent Google reviews. Look at 2–3 Instagram posts. Visit their website and look for a contact form or booking system.
The goal: walk into the call already knowing their business well enough to ask smart questions. Nothing impresses a business owner more than someone who says "I looked at your Instagram before our call and noticed that you get a lot of questions about your weekend availability." That single sentence tells them you are serious, professional, and have already done work to help them — before they have even agreed to anything.
2
Have your Make.com demo scenario open and ready to share
Open your browser and log into make.com. Go to your Scenarios list. Click on the lead follow-up automation you built in Module 2. The scenario canvas opens showing the visual flow — Google Form → OpenAI → Gmail. Have this tab ready but minimized.
When the time comes to demo during the call, you will click "Share screen" in your video call tool (Zoom, Google Meet, or similar), then switch to this tab. Even if you have only tested it with your own email, seeing a visual flowchart of the automation is powerful. It makes the abstract concept real and tangible. Prepare one test email showing a sample AI-generated reply — have it in your Gmail inbox ready to show.
3
Write down the 5 discovery questions and keep them visible
On a piece of paper or a sticky note visible to you during the call, write these 5 questions: (1) How do customers usually contact you? (2) How long does it take to reply? (3) Has a slow response ever cost you a client? (4) How many inquiries do you get per day? (5) What is a new client worth to your business? You will ask these in order in the first 10 minutes of the call.
The Call Structure — Minute by Minute (25 Minutes Total)
0–2 min
Open — Build instant rapport and set the agenda
Answer warmly, use their name, and immediately show you respect their time by getting to the point. Say exactly this:
"Hi [Name], thanks so much for making time. I know you're busy so I'll keep this to 25 minutes as I promised. I had a look at your [Google profile/Instagram] before we spoke and I noticed [specific thing]. That's actually why I reached out — I think there's a real opportunity here. Is it okay if I ask you a few quick questions first so I can make sure what I show you is relevant to your specific situation?"

They will always say yes. Now you control the structure of the call from the start.
2–12 min
Discovery — Ask the 5 questions and listen carefully
Your only job in this section is to ask questions and listen. Do not talk about your service yet. Ask questions in this order and genuinely listen to every answer — take notes if needed:
Question 1: "How do your customers usually contact you when they want to ask about your [product/service] — WhatsApp, Instagram, phone, email?"

Question 2: "How long does it usually take for you or your team to reply to a message that comes in?"

Question 3: "Has there ever been a situation where you lost a potential client just because you couldn't reply fast enough?" — wait for them to answer. This question usually opens up a story that validates the entire problem.

Question 4: "Roughly how many new inquiries do you get per week?"

Question 5 (the money question): "And just so I can show you the right numbers — roughly what is one new client worth to your business over the course of their relationship with you?" — this question is important because it lets you calculate ROI for them later.
12–18 min
Presentation — Show the solution tailored to their answers
Now present your solution, but reference what THEY told you — not a generic pitch. Say:
"So based on what you've told me — you get about [X inquiries per week] and it takes [Y hours] to reply to them — here's what I'd build for you: an AI system that replies to every [WhatsApp message / Instagram DM / contact form submission] within seconds, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. The reply sounds exactly like you — I customize it with your [menu / services / prices / style] so customers can't tell it's automated."

Then say: "Let me show you what this actually looks like — do you mind if I share my screen for 60 seconds?" Click Share Screen. Switch to your Make.com scenario tab. Show them the visual flow. Then switch to your Gmail and show a sample AI-generated reply. The entire demo takes 90 seconds and closes more deals than 10 minutes of talking.
18–22 min
ROI moment — calculate the value for them
Before quoting your price, calculate the ROI using the numbers they gave you. This makes your price feel small compared to the value:
"You said you get about [X] inquiries per week and each new client is worth about [€Y]. If this system captures even 2 extra clients per month that you would have otherwise lost to slow response times, that's [2 × Y] in extra revenue per month. My fee is [your price] per month. So the system pays for itself if it gets you just one extra client every [time period]."

Let that math sit for a moment. Then move to pricing.
22–25 min
Pricing and close — quote confidently and wait
State your price clearly and confidently. Do not soften it. Do not apologize. Just say it:
"To build and set up this system specifically for your business, it's [€X] one time. After that it's [€Y] per month for me to maintain it, update it, and make sure it keeps working perfectly. The build takes about a week from when we start. I typically ask for 50% upfront and the rest on delivery."

Then stop talking completely. Look at them. Wait. The silence feels awkward but it is normal — let them think. The first person to speak after a price quote almost always loses. Wait for their response before saying anything else.
Handling the 5 Most Common Objections — Exact Words to Use
1
"I need to think about it"
Do not panic. "I need to think about it" usually means there is a specific concern they have not said out loud. Surface it with this response:
"Of course — take the time you need. Can I ask: is there a specific aspect you want to think through? I want to make sure I've answered all your questions before we end the call."

This question almost always reveals the real objection (price, trust, technical uncertainty) which you can then address directly. If they still say "I just need a few days," say "Absolutely — I'll follow up on [specific day]. Does [day] work?" Get a specific date on the calendar before you hang up.
2
"It's too expensive"
Do not immediately lower the price — this signals your price was not real to begin with. Instead, reframe the math:
"I understand the concern. You mentioned a new client is worth about [€Y] to you. This system pays for itself if it helps you capture just one client per month you would have otherwise lost to slow response. After that, everything is profit. Does that calculation change how you see the investment?"

If they still push, you can offer a compromise: "Since you'd be one of my first clients in this specific area, I can reduce the setup fee to [€X] in exchange for a written review of the results after 30 days. The monthly retainer stays the same."
3
"Can you give me a discount?"
Discounting without getting something in return trains clients to always ask for discounts. Offer a value trade instead:
"I don't usually discount my standard pricing, but what I can offer is this: if you're willing to be a reference client and give me a written testimonial after 30 days, I'll reduce the setup fee by [€X]. The monthly rate stays as quoted. Does that work for you?"
4
"My nephew does websites — maybe he can build this"
This objection means they are not yet sold on the value of working with you specifically. Respond by establishing your specific expertise:
"That's great that you have someone technical in the family! The thing is, what I do is quite different from website building — it requires specific APIs, WhatsApp Business verification, OpenAI account setup, and ongoing prompt engineering to keep the AI responses accurate. Most web developers don't specialize in this stack. Plus, with me you get a done-for-you system with ongoing maintenance — not a one-time build you then manage yourself. But if they want to try, absolutely worth seeing if they can do it — and if you ever need help, I'm here."
5
"Can I see it working first before I pay?"
This is a great sign — they want it, they just want proof. Give them proof immediately:
"Absolutely — give me your WhatsApp number and I'll send you a test message right now so you can see what your customers would experience."

Then, live on the call, send their number a message through your test automation. Wait 10–15 seconds. The AI reply arrives. They see it happen in real time. This closes more deals than any amount of talking or slide presentations.
After They Say Yes — Collecting Payment and Starting Work
1
Send the invoice immediately after the call — within 5 minutes
While they are still in the "yes" mindset, send the payment link immediately. Do not wait until tomorrow. Go to dashboard.stripe.com — click Payment Links in the left sidebar → click + New → enter the setup fee amount → click Create Link → copy the link → paste it into a WhatsApp message to the client.
The message to send: "Great speaking with you [Name]! Here's the link to pay the 50% setup deposit — [Stripe link]. Once payment is confirmed, I'll send you an intake form to collect all the details I need to start building. Looking forward to it!" If they don't pay within 48 hours, follow up once. If still no payment, the deal was not as closed as you thought — go back to the objection handling conversation.

Send the client intake form within 24 hours of receiving payment
Once you receive the deposit, send them your Google Form intake questionnaire. Go to forms.google.com. You should have already created this form (if not, do it now). The form asks for: their business name exactly as it should appear, their services/products list, their brand voice description, their frequently asked customer questions, their WhatsApp number, their operating hours, and any specific instructions for the bot.
Message to send with the form: "Payment received, thank you! Here's a short form to fill in — it gives me everything I need to build your system: [Google Form link]. Should take about 10 minutes. Once I have your responses, I'll have your automation ready within 5–7 days."

Deliver and collect final payment
After building and testing the automation, schedule a 30-minute delivery call. Share your screen and show the automation running live with their real business details. Let them ask questions. After the call, send the final 50% invoice via Stripe. Once paid, set up their monthly recurring billing so future payments happen automatically.
Your First Client Strategy
For your very first client, consider offering the setup at a reduced price ($100–$150) or even free, in exchange for a monthly retainer and a written testimonial after 30 days. One specific case study — "Restaurant X received 40% faster replies and booked 5 extra tables in the first week" — is worth more than any amount of cold outreach. It becomes your most powerful sales tool for every client after. One good testimonial closes deals you could never close with cold messages alone.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 3
Spend the first 10 minutes asking questions — not pitching. Their answers tell you exactly how to close the deal.
A 90-second live demo closes more deals than 10 minutes of talking. Have your Make.com scenario ready to share on screen.
Calculate ROI using their own numbers before quoting price. Make the math obvious — one recovered client pays for months of your retainer.
Send the invoice within 5 minutes of hanging up. The longer you wait, the more likely the deal falls through.
