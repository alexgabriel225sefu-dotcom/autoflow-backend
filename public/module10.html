Part A — Twilio Setup (Complete Walkthrough)
1
Go to twilio.com and click Sign Up
Open your browser and navigate to twilio.com. In the top-right corner, click the Sign Up button. A form appears with four fields. Fill in: First Name, Last Name, Email address, and Password. Click Start your free trial. Twilio will send you a verification email.
2
Verify your email address
Check your inbox for an email from Twilio with the subject "Confirm your email to get started." Open the email. Click the blue Confirm Email button. Your browser opens and confirms your email is verified. You return to the Twilio signup flow.
If the email does not arrive within 2 minutes, check your spam folder. The sender address is noreply@twilio.com. If it is still missing, return to twilio.com and click "Resend verification email."
3
Answer the onboarding questions
Twilio shows a brief survey to personalize your setup. Answer each question: for "What are you building?" select Voice. For "What coding language?" select Node.js. For "Are you starting a new project?" click Yes. Click Get Started with Twilio. You land on the Twilio Console — the main control panel for your account.
4
Get a Twilio phone number
This is the number clients' customers will call. In the Twilio Console left sidebar, find and click Phone Numbers. A submenu expands. Click Manage, then click Buy a number. A search page appears. Select your country from the dropdown. Under the "Capabilities" filter, make sure the Voice checkbox is checked. Click Search. A list of available numbers appears with their monthly costs.
Phone numbers cost approximately $1/month each. Local numbers (matching the caller's country code) convert better than international numbers. If you are building for a UK business, get a UK number. For US businesses, get a US number.
5
Purchase the number and confirm
Click Buy next to any available number in the list. A confirmation dialog appears showing the number, its monthly cost, and which capabilities it includes. Confirm that "Voice" is listed under capabilities. Click Buy This Number. The purchase completes immediately. You see a success message: "Your new phone number is [number]."
6
Find and save your Twilio credentials
Click the Twilio logo in the top-left to return to the Console home. Near the top of the page you see two pieces of information: Account SID (starts with "AC" followed by 32 characters) and Auth Token (hidden by default). Click Show next to Auth Token to reveal it. Copy both the Account SID and Auth Token and save them in a password manager or secure note. You will need these in Lesson 2.
Treat the Auth Token like a password. Anyone who has it can make calls and send messages charged to your account. Never paste it into a GitHub repository or share it in a chat.
Part B — ElevenLabs Setup (Complete Walkthrough)
1
Go to elevenlabs.io and sign up
Open a new browser tab and navigate to elevenlabs.io. Click Sign Up in the top-right corner. You can register with your Google account (fastest) or with an email and password. If using email: enter your email, create a password, and click Create Account. ElevenLabs sends a verification email — click the link inside it to verify. After verification, you land on the ElevenLabs dashboard.
2
Get your API Key
In the ElevenLabs dashboard, look at the top-right corner and click your profile icon (a circle with your initials or profile picture). A dropdown menu appears. Click Profile. The profile settings page opens. Scroll down until you see a section called API Key. Your API key is shown as a partially hidden string. Click the Copy button next to it. Paste the key into your secure notes — you need it in Lesson 2.
If you see a "Reveal" button instead of the key, click it first to show the full key, then copy it. The key is a long string of letters and numbers — make sure you copy the entire thing.
3
Choose a voice for the agent
In the ElevenLabs left sidebar, click Voice Library. This page shows dozens of available voices. Each voice has a small play button — click it to hear a preview of that voice speaking. For professional business use, listen to voices like "Rachel," "Adam," "Josh," or "Elli." They sound natural and clear on phone calls.
For customer-facing business agents: choose a voice that sounds calm, warm, and professional. Avoid voices that sound young or overly enthusiastic — they can feel off-putting in a customer service context. Listen to at least 5 voices before deciding.
4
Add the voice to your Voice Lab and copy its Voice ID
When you find the voice you want, click on it. A panel slides in from the right showing the voice details. Click Add to Voice Lab. Now click Voice Lab in the left sidebar. You see the voice you just added. Click on it. Look for its Voice ID — a string of letters and numbers shown below the voice name (example: 21m00Tcm4TlvDq8ikWAM). Copy it and save it with your other credentials.
5
Test the voice before using it
Still in Voice Lab, find the voice you added. There is a text field below the voice name. Click inside it and type:
Hello, thank you for calling. How can I help you today?
Click Generate. After a few seconds, a play button appears. Click it and listen. This is exactly how the voice agent will sound to callers. If the voice sounds right, you are ready for Lesson 2. If not, go back to Voice Library and try a different voice.
Why ElevenLabs Instead of Other Text-to-Speech Services
Amazon Polly, Google TTS, and even Twilio's built-in TTS sound robotic. ElevenLabs produces voices that are indistinguishable from real humans in most phone call contexts. This quality difference is what justifies the $1,500–$3,000 price tag when selling to clients. When a prospect calls your demo number and hears the ElevenLabs voice, the deal closes itself — you cannot achieve that reaction with robotic TTS.

Build · Lesson 2

Build the Voice Agent — Complete Code

This lesson gives you the complete working server code. You create a GitHub repository, add the files, deploy to Render, set your environment variables, then connect Twilio to the server. When you are done, calling the Twilio number will trigger the AI agent.

Step 1 — Create the GitHub Repository
1
Go to github.com and create a new repository
Open github.com and log in. Click the + icon in the top-right corner. In the dropdown, click New repository. The "Create a new repository" page opens. In the "Repository name" field, type voice-agent. Leave the repository set to Public. Do not add a README or .gitignore. Click Create repository. The repository is created and you land on the empty repo page.
2
Create the server.js file
On the empty repository page, click creating a new file (the link in the center of the page). The file editor opens. In the filename field at the top, type server.js. Click inside the large text editor area below. Paste this complete code:
const express = require('express'); const twilio = require('twilio'); const axios = require('axios'); const app = express(); app.use(express.urlencoded({ extended: true })); app.use(express.json()); const OPENAI_KEY = process.env.OPENAI_API_KEY; const ELEVENLABS_KEY = process.env.ELEVENLABS_API_KEY; const VOICE_ID = process.env.ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID || '21m00Tcm4TlvDq8ikWAM'; const BUSINESS_NAME = process.env.BUSINESS_NAME || 'our business'; const BUSINESS_INFO = process.env.BUSINESS_INFO || 'We are a professional business.'; const calls = {}; app.post('/voice', (req, res) => { const callSid = req.body.CallSid; calls[callSid] = []; const twiml = new twilio.twiml.VoiceResponse(); const greeting = `Hello! Welcome to ${BUSINESS_NAME}. I'm your AI assistant. How can I help you today?`; twiml.say({ voice: 'alice' }, greeting); twiml.gather({ input: 'speech', action: '/process', speechTimeout: 'auto' }); res.type('text/xml'); res.send(twiml.toString()); }); app.post('/process', async (req, res) => { const callSid = req.body.CallSid; const userSpeech = req.body.SpeechResult || ''; if (!calls[callSid]) calls[callSid] = []; calls[callSid].push({ role: 'user', content: userSpeech }); const messages = [ { role: 'system', content: `You are a helpful phone assistant for ${BUSINESS_NAME}. ${BUSINESS_INFO} Keep replies under 2 sentences. Be friendly and professional.` }, ...calls[callSid] ]; const aiRes = await axios.post('https://api.openai.com/v1/chat/completions', { model: 'gpt-4o', messages, max_tokens: 150 }, { headers: { Authorization: `Bearer ${OPENAI_KEY}` } }); const reply = aiRes.data.choices[0].message.content; calls[callSid].push({ role: 'assistant', content: reply }); const twiml = new twilio.twiml.VoiceResponse(); twiml.say({ voice: 'alice' }, reply); twiml.gather({ input: 'speech', action: '/process', speechTimeout: 'auto' }); res.type('text/xml'); res.send(twiml.toString()); }); app.listen(process.env.PORT || 3000);
After pasting, scroll down to the bottom of the page. You will see a "Commit new file" section. Leave the commit message as the default ("Create server.js") or type your own. Click Commit new file. The file is saved to the repository.
3
Create the package.json file
You are now viewing the repository with server.js in it. Click Add file (top right, near the green "Code" button). Click Create new file. In the filename field, type package.json. Paste this into the editor:
{ "name": "voice-agent", "version": "1.0.0", "main": "server.js", "scripts": { "start": "node server.js" }, "dependencies": { "express": "^4.18.2", "twilio": "^4.19.0", "axios": "^1.6.0" }, "engines": { "node": ">=18.0.0" } }
Scroll down and click Commit new file. Your repository now has 2 files: server.js and package.json. This is everything Render needs to deploy the agent.
Step 2 — Deploy to Render
4
Go to render.com and sign up or log in
Open render.com. If you do not have an account, click Get Started for Free and sign up with your GitHub account — this is the easiest option because Render will automatically see your GitHub repositories. After signing in, you land on the Render Dashboard.
5
Create a new Web Service
On the Render Dashboard, click the New + button in the top-right corner. A dropdown appears. Click Web Service. The "Create a new Web Service" page opens. Under "Connect a repository," you see a list of your GitHub repositories. Find voice-agent in the list and click Connect next to it.
6
Configure the service settings
A configuration form appears. Fill in each field: In "Name" type voice-agent-[clientname] (replace [clientname] with your client's business name, no spaces). Under "Environment" confirm it shows Node. In "Build Command" type npm install. In "Start Command" type npm start. Leave "Plan" on the free tier for now.
7
Add Environment Variables
Scroll down to find the Environment Variables section. Click Add Environment Variable five times to create five rows. Fill them in exactly as follows — key on the left, value on the right:
Key: OPENAI_API_KEY    Value: your OpenAI API key (from platform.openai.com)

Key: ELEVENLABS_API_KEY    Value: your ElevenLabs API key from Lesson 1

Key: ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID    Value: the Voice ID you copied in Lesson 1

Key: BUSINESS_NAME    Value: the client's business name (e.g., "Sunshine Dental")

Key: BUSINESS_INFO    Value: a detailed description — services, hours, location, FAQ answers, booking process
BUSINESS_INFO is what the AI knows about the client's business. Write it as a paragraph: "Sunshine Dental is a dental clinic located at 45 Oak Street, London. We are open Monday–Friday 9am–6pm and Saturday 9am–1pm. We offer cleanings, fillings, crowns, and whitening. New patients book online at sunshinedentaluk.com or call this number."
8
Click "Create Web Service" and wait for deployment
Scroll to the bottom of the configuration page and click Create Web Service. Render begins deploying. You see a log stream showing the build process. Wait 2–3 minutes. When you see "Your service is live" or the status changes to green, the deployment is complete. Look at the top of the page — you see a URL that looks like https://voice-agent-xxx.onrender.com. Copy this URL.
Step 3 — Connect Twilio to the Render Server
9
Go to the Twilio Console and find your phone number
Open console.twilio.com. In the left sidebar, click Phone Numbers. The submenu expands. Click Manage, then click Active Numbers. You see the phone number you purchased in Lesson 1. Click on the phone number itself (the number is a clickable link).
10
Set the Voice webhook URL
The phone number configuration page opens. Scroll down until you find the section labeled Voice & Fax. Inside it, find the row that says "A CALL COMES IN." Make sure the first dropdown shows Webhook. In the URL field next to it, paste your Render URL with /voice appended:
https://voice-agent-xxx.onrender.com/voice
Change the HTTP method dropdown to HTTP POST. Scroll to the bottom of the page and click Save. You see a success banner confirming the changes were saved.
11
Call the Twilio number and test the agent
Pick up your phone. Dial the Twilio number you purchased. After 1–2 rings, the AI agent answers. You hear the greeting: "Hello! Welcome to [BUSINESS_NAME]. I'm your AI assistant. How can I help you today?" Speak a question. Wait 1–2 seconds. The agent responds with a relevant answer based on the BUSINESS_INFO you provided.
Test with these questions:

"What are your opening hours?" — the agent should answer from BUSINESS_INFO
"Where are you located?" — the agent should give the address
"How do I book an appointment?" — the agent should explain the booking process
"What services do you offer?" — the agent should list the services

If the agent answers correctly for all four, the setup is complete. If it gives generic answers, improve your BUSINESS_INFO environment variable in Render → Environment tab → update the value → click Save → the service redeploys in 1–2 minutes.
Your Voice Agent is Now Live
Anyone who calls the Twilio number will reach the AI agent. It answers 24/7, never puts callers on hold, and handles the same questions a human receptionist would. This is the most impressive demo you can show a client. During your next sales call, say "call this number right now" and hand them the Twilio number. Watch their reaction when the agent answers naturally.
Key Takeaways — Lesson 2
The agent lives on Render — one Web Service per client, each with its own BUSINESS_NAME and BUSINESS_INFO.
BUSINESS_INFO is the agent's knowledge base — write it in detail. The more you include, the better the agent performs.
Twilio connects to the agent via the /voice webhook URL — change this in the Phone Number settings.
Test with at least 10 questions before delivering to a client — edge cases surface in testing, not in front of the client.
Sales · Lesson 3

How to Sell the Voice Agent for $1,500–$3,000

The voice agent is the highest-converting product you can sell. A prospect who hears an AI handling a phone call naturally does not need persuading — they ask how soon you can set it up. This lesson covers targeting, the live demo, pricing, and handling objections.

Who Needs a Voice Agent Most
1
Medical and dental clinics
A dental clinic gets 50–150 calls per day. Approximately 70% of those calls are the same 6 questions: booking an appointment, cancelling, asking about prices, directions, opening hours, and insurance. The receptionist spends 3–4 hours answering these. The voice agent handles all of them instantly — the receptionist only picks up calls that are genuinely complex.
Pitch: "Your receptionist spends 3–4 hours per day answering the same questions on repeat. My AI agent handles all of that — instantly, 24/7, even when you're closed and a patient calls at 9pm wondering what to do about a toothache. Setup is $2,000 and $300/month to maintain."
2
Restaurants taking reservations
Restaurants miss the most calls during their busiest hours — dinner service — precisely when every staff member is occupied. The voice agent takes reservations, confirms them, answers menu questions, and notes dietary requirements. Every missed call is a missed table booking.
3
Customer support businesses
Any business with a support line that receives repetitive calls benefits from a voice agent. E-commerce stores handling returns, insurance brokers handling policy questions, real estate agencies handling property inquiry calls — all are strong prospects.
The Demo That Closes Every Deal
4
Before the call — configure the demo for their industry
Go to your Render dashboard. Click on your voice-agent service. Click Environment. Update BUSINESS_NAME to a realistic name for their industry: "Westfield Dental" or "Milano Restaurant." Update BUSINESS_INFO with realistic details for that type of business. Click Save Changes. Wait 2 minutes for the service to redeploy. Call the number to confirm the new setup is working before your sales call.
5
On the call — deliver the live demo
When the time is right in the call (usually after discussing the problem), say:
I want to show you something live right now. I've built a demo for a business similar to yours. Can you call this number from your phone while we're on the call? [Give them the Twilio number.] Don't tell me what you're going to ask it — just ask it whatever a real customer would ask.
They call the number. The agent answers. They ask something. The agent responds naturally. There is usually a pause. Then they say something like "that's incredible" or "I didn't know this was possible." That pause is where the deal closes — do not speak during it. Let them process what they just experienced.
6
After the demo — quote the price
After the demo, say:
What you just experienced is what your customers would hear every time they call — at 2am on a Sunday, during your busiest lunch hour, even when every staff member is busy. I set this up in under a day. To build it specifically for your business, set up is $2,000 and maintenance is $300 per month. Most clients recover the cost in the first 3 weeks from the appointments the agent books after hours.
Pricing Tiers and Customization Add-ons
7
Basic — $800 setup / $200/month
Answers calls, handles FAQs, takes messages. Build time: 2–3 hours. Uses BUSINESS_INFO for all answers. No external integrations. This is your entry-level offer for small businesses or clients who want to test before committing.
8
Advanced — $1,500 setup / $350/month
Everything in Basic plus booking integration (Google Calendar or Calendly), CRM logging (Airtable or Notion), and conversation summaries emailed to the business owner. Build time: 5–7 hours. This is the most commonly purchased tier.
9
Enterprise — $3,000 setup / $500/month
Everything in Advanced plus multilingual support (the agent detects language and responds in kind), custom voice clone (using ElevenLabs voice cloning to sound like the business owner), priority escalation routing, and weekly performance reports. Build time: 10–14 hours.
10
Handling the "Will customers know it's AI?" objection
This question comes up in approximately 60% of sales calls. The honest answer is: yes, it is AI, and most customers do not mind — they care about getting an answer, not about who answers. Address it directly:
Legally and ethically, yes — it's an AI agent. Practically, most customers only notice if the agent cannot help them. And for the questions your customers ask 80% of the time — hours, booking, pricing, location — the agent handles perfectly. We always set up an escalation path: if a caller asks something the agent can't answer, it immediately offers to take a message and have a human call back within [X] minutes. Your customers get faster answers for routine questions and faster callbacks for complex ones.
Do Not Skip the Live Demo
A recorded demo (video or audio clip) converts at roughly 20% of the rate of a live demo. The difference is real-time involvement — the prospect calls the number themselves and asks a real question. They are not watching your experience, they are having their own. Every time you skip the live demo and rely on description or slides, you leave money on the table. Build the demo agent first. Demo it on every call. Period.
